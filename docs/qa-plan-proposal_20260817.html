QA Plan Proposal — full-coverage testing of the GL rating engine

ISO General Liability · Recursive Harness 2.0 · 17 August 2026 · Proposal only — nothing in this document has been built
What this proposes. A test programme that covers all 51 jurisdictions across multiple locations, multiple classifications, varied limits, varied classes and varied exposure amounts — sized from ISO's own declared content rather than from guesswork, run either from a terminal command or a single button, reviewed automatically by the harness itself, and reported in a form a non-technical reader can act on.

The central recommendation. Do not build one test matrix. Build two, with different purposes and very different sizes — a value sweep that checks every filed rate comes back correctly, and a logic matrix that checks the engine behaves correctly. Conflating them is what makes people propose a cross-product of 1.9 × 1016 and then never run it.

3 · The two test objectives, sized separately

This is the structural decision the whole plan rests on. There are two different questions, and they need different machinery.

A · Value coverage — "is every filed rate returned correctly?"

278,054filed rate cells to verify

A data-comparison job, not a scenario matrix. ERC 217,461 premises/operations loss-cost cells and 60,593 products cells across the 51 jurisdictions — exactly classes × territories, no gaps.

One rating per (state, territory, class). Mostly mechanical, highly parallelisable, and it answers a question a carrier will absolutely ask: does it get my class right?

B · Logic coverage — "does the engine behave correctly?"

~2,000–4,000scenarios, pairwise over binned axes

A scenario matrix. This is where deductible ordering, limit keying, multi-location aggregation, minimum premiums and the rating plans get exercised.

The number is small because the axes were measured: most of them are keyed on each other, so the honest degrees of freedom are far fewer than the raw cardinalities suggest.

Why not a cross-product. ERC The full cross product for a single subline at one location and one classification is 1.94 × 1016. That figure is not a target — it is the argument against the design. Quoting it, and then quoting the 464 legal limit combinations that replace 11,700 naive ones, is how this plan justifies a sample instead of an enumeration.

4.3 Sampling class codes — the measurement that makes it tractable

ERC Grouping every class code by its actual rating behaviour — premium basis, both ILF table assignments, and both size-of-risk table assignments — gives:

  • 1,188 class codes → 558 distinct behaviour classes
  • 376 of the 558 are singletons — one class code each
  • Constituents: 59 bases, 4 prem/ops ILF assignments, 5 products assignments, 84 prem/ops size-of-risk tables, 43 products tables
The tail is the trap. Gross Sales, Payroll and Area cover 87% of class codes (685 / 268 / 82). But 49 of the 59 premium bases carry five class codes or fewer, and 33 carry exactly one — "No Exposure", "Vehicles (Toll Bridges)", "Passenger Days". A sample drawn uniformly at random would effectively never touch them. The plan therefore draws stratified by behaviour class, not by class code.

4.4 Deductibles — three shapes, not a grid

ERC 31 legal values per side (No Deductible, plus 15 amounts × {Per Claim, Per Occurrence}). The combined BI/PD domain has 991 rows, and they decompose exactly: 960 of the 961 (BI, PD) pairs admit only No Deductible as the combined value; only the (none, none) pair admits all 31.

So the deductible axis is three structural cases, not a free grid:

  1. Split BI and PD set, combined necessarily none
  2. Combined set, both splits necessarily none
  3. Nothing set

Sampled at 3 shapes × 6 amounts × 2 sides (prem/ops, products, independent) = 36 deductible configurations, plus the refer-to-company band above $5,000 MANUAL which must be tested as a referral, not as a premium.

4.5 Exposure — 98 bands and a gate

ERC Size-of-risk relativity is interpolated over 98 bands (8,330 prem/ops rows, 4,214 products rows), from 1,000 up to a top band closing at the 64-bit maximum. Two things gate it:

  1. SizeOfRiskRatingApplies must be Yes, or the band key is zero and banding never fires.
  2. The band key is scaled by premium basis. For nine bases the key is floor(exposure/1000) × 1000; for the other fifty it is exposure × 1000. So band 1,000–2,000 means $1,000–$2,000 of sales on Gross Sales but 1–2 units on a count basis.

Proposed exposure sample: three points per band boundary — edge − 1, edge, edge + 1 — on a rotating subset of bands, plus both scaling regimes. Boundary-adjacent values are where interpolation and banding defects live; a midpoint tells you almost nothing.

A defect found while measuring this. CODE ISO declares an exposure range of 1 … 9,999,999,999. Our engine reads that bound and discards it — MinimumValue/MaximumValue appear nowhere in gl_engine/, and Field.minimum/.maximum are parsed but read by nothing. We accept any exposure at all. Listed as D-4 in section 9.

4.6 Effective date — the axis that does not exist

The most consequential finding in this proposal. MANUAL All 51 jurisdictions are on the pre-2027 basis today. 43 migrate on a single morning — 1 April 2027. It is not a renumbering: Rule 14, classification-level minimum premiums, is deleted outright — the per-subline, highest-of apparatus, the "if any" provision and ~25 special combined mercantile classes all vanish. Premium changes for small risks. The class list also moves from 1,188 to 1,163 — 25 codes withdrawn.

CODE Our harness has no as-of axis. sweep.py has no --asof flag, the tester never sends one, and the rating date comes from the payload while the options come from a separately-passed as-of — so setting one today changes which values are offered while the engine still rates at 2026-08-01. That is a live inconsistency, not merely a missing feature.

Recommendation: effective date becomes a first-class axis, and every fixture is pinned to (jurisdiction, as-of) — never to a global class list.

5 · How tests are triggered

Two routes, one engine underneath. Both already have most of their plumbing.

5.1 From the terminal

NEW A single qa entry point wrapping the existing runners, so a tier is one command:

# run a whole tier
python scripts/qa.py --tier T1                    # ~600 scenarios, live
python scripts/qa.py --tier T1 --offline          # free, no ISO calls
python scripts/qa.py --tier T0                    # smoke, all 51

# narrow it
python scripts/qa.py --tier T2 --juris NY --juris CA
python scripts/qa.py --tier T2 --axis deductible --axis limits
python scripts/qa.py --tier T4 --asof 20270401    # the edition cliff

# what would it do, and what would it cost?
python scripts/qa.py --tier T2 --plan             # print the matrix, run nothing
python scripts/qa.py --estimate                   # calls, wall-clock, per tier

# re-run only what failed last time
python scripts/qa.py --rerun-failures <run-id>
Design rule. --plan and --estimate are not conveniences. A tier that makes thousands of billable calls must be able to show exactly what it intends to do before it does it, and every run must report calls spent alongside results.

7 · Recursive harness review of the results

The harness reviews its own output before a human sees it. This is the part of the proposal with the most evidence behind it — of the six defects resolved on 17 August 2026, every one was found by running something against something else, and one was a defect in the measuring apparatus itself.

7.1 Four review passes, in order

PassQuestionStatus
1 · Did it exercise anything? For every scenario whose premium equalled the base: is this ISO's filing doing nothing (INERT CONTROL) or did we pick a value that does nothing (INERT VALUE)? CODE Exists — probe_no_op
2 · Is a refusal correct? When our engine refuses, does ISO refuse too — and for the same reason? A refusal that matches for the wrong reason is not agreement NEW
3 · Is a "not applicable" real? Re-derive the undeclarability from ISO's declaration independently. This is the pass that would have caught OI-91, where twenty jurisdictions were refused for three days by our own rule wearing a well-written explanation NEW
4 · Adversarial read The specialist agents read the run and try to refute the result: gl-authority against ISO's content, iso-circular-expert against the filed manual, gl-engine-code-expert against our own source NEW
Pass 3 exists because of a real failure. The harness reported NOT APPLICABLE in twenty jurisdictions with a reason attached, and that is exactly why nobody questioned it. It was our own fallback, not ISO's filing. A refusal with a good explanation is still a refusal, and nothing was checking the explanation.

7.2 What the review emits

Harness review · T1 run 2026-08-17 02:00 · 3 findings
Finding 1 — needs a human · confirmed by 3 of 3 reviewers
NY · two locations · $2M/$4M · products deductible $5,000ours 41,208 · ISO 41,190 · $18

In plain English: with two locations in different territories and a products deductible, our premium is $18 high in New York only.
What the reviewers said: gl-authority — the products deductible factor is keyed to the class's ILF table assignment, and the two locations resolve different assignments; iso-circular-expert — Rule 15.D.2 requires the factor to follow the table assignment, not the deductible amount; gl-engine-code-expert — our applier sets one factor for the whole risk.
Suspected cause: deductible factor resolved once per risk instead of once per classification.

Finding 2 — exercised nothing
NY · terrorism onINERT VALUE

Territory 001 carries no terrorism charge in NY; 002 would have moved the premium by 110. The scenario reported as tested and tested nothing. Auto-fix available: re-draw the territory.

Finding 3 — agreement, but not evidence
OK · schedule rating onINERT CONTROL

No declared value moves the premium in Oklahoma — that is ISO's own credibility gate, not our defect. Recorded so this scenario is never counted as coverage of schedule rating.

Wireframe 6 — the review. Every finding carries a plain-English sentence, the independent reviewers' verdicts, and a suspected cause. Agreement that proves nothing is reported as loudly as disagreement.

8 · Analysis of results

8.1 The four outcomes — and why three-way beats pass/fail

OutcomeMeansCounts as
AgreesSame premium and every published figure matchesSuccess
DiffersPremium or a published figure differsOur defect until proven otherwise
Not applicableISO's filing does not offer this combination in this stateNeither — never a failure
Both refuseWe decline and ISO declines, same reasonAgreement, counted separately from "agrees"
Why "both refuse" is not folded into the pass count. It is genuine agreement — but calling a refusal a match inflates the number that matters. On 17 August a seven-jurisdiction run reported 181 matches + 3 mutual refusals + 3 not-applicable = 184 of 184 agreeing, with the categories kept apart. That is the reporting standard this plan adopts.

8.2 The analyses each run produces

  • Did coverage grow? Scenarios newly exercised, and axis values still never touched.
  • Did anything regress? Defects keyed by (state, status, configuration) with first-seen and last-seen — CODE the register already exists.
  • Is a difference systematic or isolated? One state at $18 is a state rule; twelve states at a constant ratio is a factor applied in the wrong place.
  • Does the premium respond sensibly? Monotonic in limits, descending in deductibles, linear between exposure band edges.
  • How much did it cost, and what did it buy? Calls spent against defects found and coverage gained — the number that decides whether a tier keeps earning its place.

8.3 Reading a difference — the triage order

  1. Is the scenario legal? If ISO would reject the submission, the difference is ours to fix in the harness, not the engine.
  2. Is it one state or many? One → a state exception. Many → a countrywide rule.
  3. Is it constant or proportional? A fixed amount → an additive charge. A ratio → a factor.
  4. What does the trace say? Every rating returns the full factor chain with each value's source in ISO's files.
  5. Does the manual agree with the content? Where they disagree, that is the finding.

12.2 The economics — why limited scale can still be robust

Live calls buy new truth. Offline runs defend it.
A live call is needed once per fact. The moment ISO confirms a configuration produces 8,816, that becomes a stored golden and is checked free, on every run, forever. Re-asking the same question weekly buys nothing except drift detection.

An offline rating costs 0.5 s and nothing. A live one costs 8.4 s and money. So the rule is: spend calls on establishing truth, spend compute on defending it.

Three reasons a small live budget still gives real confidence:

  1. Offline localises defects; the oracle only confirms them. On 17 August every defect was found and its blast radius measured offline before a single call was spent — 14 jurisdictions identified from traces, then 13 calls to confirm.
  2. Self-consistency needs no oracle. Premium must rise with limits, fall with deductibles and interpolate linearly between band edges. A kink is a defect whether or not ISO is asked.
  3. Mechanisms generalise; values do not. Deductible ordering is one mechanism, confirmable in a handful of states. A rate is state-specific across 278,054 cells and generalises to nothing — which is why those are verified offline against ISO's files rather than by call.

12.4 In plain English — what we can test, and what we cannot

Can test properly

  • Every rate ISO filed — 217,461 premises/operations and 60,593 products cells, offline, against ISO's own files.
  • The arithmetic — deductibles, limits, increased-limit factors, size-of-risk banding, territory assignment, aggregation across locations and classes.
  • All 50 comparable jurisdictions against a live independent oracle, on the premium and every published figure — not just the total.
  • Refusals — that we decline what ISO declines, and for the same reason.
  • That a test tested something — the difference between "rated" and "exercised".
  • Regression — that today's answer matches yesterday's, everywhere, for free.

Can test only partly, today

  • The other sublines — Liquor, contractors, pollution, railroad and the rest. 7 of 11 have no starting payload; buildable, but real work per family.
  • Multi-location and multi-class — no payloads today, and 20 jurisdictions cannot host multi-location at all because they declare a single territory.
  • The 2027 rules — 43 states change basis next April with minimum premiums deleted outright, and we have no effective-date axis to test either side.

Cannot test, and will not be able to

  • Puerto Rico — rates fine, no ISO entitlement. Every figure is honestly n of 50.
  • Hawaii — not in ISO's corpus at all.
  • The final premium a policyholder pays. Everything ISO files is pre-multiplier; the carrier's loss cost multiplier is an input by design.
  • Amounts ISO leaves to the carrier — policywriting minimums, classification minimums, anything marked refer to company. We can test that we refer; not the number.
  • Whether ISO itself is right. ISO is the oracle — if ISO is wrong, we agree with a wrong answer. It happens: three jurisdictions returned a 400 from ISO's own rule engine failing a lookup in its own table. The only partial defence is the second corpus — where the machine-readable content and the filed manual disagree, that is the finding.
  • Carrier deviations, once built. The moment carrier content is layered on, no external service can confirm the answer — the check that found every defect so far stops working.
  • Schedule rating, experience credibility and size-of-risk against the manual — they are not in the manual corpus at all. ISO's service will rate them, so they are testable, but there is only ever one source and no cross-check.

13.3 If ISO does ask

The honest answer is short, and worth having ready:

  • We are a licensed subscriber validating our implementation of ISO's own filed content against ISO's own service.
  • Traffic is strictly serial — no concurrency, one request at a time.
  • Under 150 requests a day, business hours, weekdays only.
  • Every request is a schema-valid submission built from ISO's own declared legal values — we do not send anything ISO's filing would reject, and invalid combinations are refused by our harness before they reach the service.
  • We never re-ask a question already answered; confirmed results are cached permanently.
  • Findings flow back. Three jurisdictions returned a 400 from ISO's own rule engine on a missing loss-cost row — that is reportable to ISO as a data issue, and we should report it.
Worth proposing to ISO proactively rather than waiting to be asked. A short note describing the validation programme, the daily ceiling and the serial pattern turns unusual traffic into an expected activity — and opens the door to raising the PremOpsSizeOfRiskLossCost gap as a content question rather than a support ticket.

14 · What this proposal does not claim

  • It is a proposal. Nothing here has been built — no qa.py, no QA tab, no new payloads, no review passes. This document and its wireframes are the entire deliverable.
  • Counts are as-of 1 August 2026. The 2027 filing withdraws 25 class codes (1,188 → 1,163) and deletes Rule 14. Any fixture list pinned to today's date will be wrong next April.
  • One genuine source disagreement is unresolved — New York, 1,191 ERC vs 1,181 manual, at the same vintage. It is reported, not reconciled.
  • Three answers are unverifiable from the corpora and need a live call or an ISO decision: the exposure ceiling, PA territory 506, and the maximum locations per submission.
  • Schedule rating, experience credibility and size-of-risk have no manual authority. They exist only in ISO's machine-readable content, so for those the live service is the only oracle — there is no second source to check against.
  • Scenario counts for T1–T4 are design targets, not measurements. They follow from the stratification in section 4, and they will move once the pairwise generator exists and the not-applicable rate is known per axis.
  • The daily ceiling and pacing figures in section 13 are our own policy, not an ISO-published limit. No rate limit is documented in our client or in the subscription terms we hold; 150/day is chosen to stay comparable to established usage, not to satisfy a known rule.
  • Wall-clock assumes serial calls, because that is what exists today. Parallelism would change every figure in 11.2 and must be agreed with ISO before it is used.
