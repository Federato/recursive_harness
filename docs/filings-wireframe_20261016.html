How each insurer prices differently from ISO

Insurers file their own changes to ISO's rates with each state. See at a glance who has filed where and how far each one moves a premium, then open a filing for the detail.

What has each insurer filed?

One card per filing. The big number is how much a typical premium moves against ISO for each company. Click a card to open it below.

Which company is dearest, and where?

Each company against each line and state, shaded by how far its typical premium sits from ISO. "ISO's rates" means that company has no filing there. Click a box to open the filing.

When does each filing take effect?

The light bar starts on the date for new policies; the dark bar on the date for renewals. Policies on or after these dates use the insurer's rates.

What changes from today's page
  • Three high-level views replace the "who has filed where" grid: filings as cards with the headline effect, companies as a shaded grid, and a timeline of effective dates.
  • Six tabs become five: In short (with the headline numbers), What changes, Effect on premium, Test it, Paper trail. Our review, History and Documents become one paper trail with the proof at the top.
  • Test it is three quick checks, not the full ISO sweep: (1) each filed factor applies as written, (2) the effect on typical premiums is within a point of what we expect (the figure recorded when it went in use, which you can overwrite, plus the carrier's own filed figure where it gives one, as a sense check), (3) an optional ISO spot check of our technical price (ISO never sees the insurer's factors, D21), one request per state, behind a confirmation. "Try one" prices a single typical risk both ways. One button runs the free checks on every filing, and each card shows when it was last tested.
  • What changes is one table, companies side by side, current version only. Earlier versions are folded away. Today each version repeats the whole table.
  • The effect on premium is shown as bars by type of business, coloured by how far above or below ISO, instead of a table of percentages.
  • Uploading moves to the side panel as a drop zone, with the four steps a filing goes through and the glossary.
  • Technical details stays one switch: it adds SERFF numbers, versions, multipliers and file fingerprints in place. (In this wireframe, multipliers other than the headline ones quoted in each filing's summary are back-worked from rounded percentages; the build reads the real ones.)
