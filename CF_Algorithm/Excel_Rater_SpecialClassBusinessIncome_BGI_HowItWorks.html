How Special Class Business Income Basic Group I Rating Works

Written: 2026-09-15. Source: CommercialPropertySpecialClassBusnIncomeRules.Rule.xml and CommercialPropertySpecialClassBusnIncomeBasicGroupICoverageRules.Rule.xml, read directly this session, plus a 46-state sweep of the rate tables and the state rule files. Companion to the four building-side pages — this is the first page on the Business Income datadef group, which is a separate group from the Special Class item itself.

Status: traced, not built. No Business Income rows exist on Special Class Worksheet.

1. What's different about this chain, in plain English

It does not compute a rate of its own. Step 1 is a straight Copy — BasicGroupIRate is lifted from the parent Special Class item (../../BasicGroupIRate), the building-side rate you have already built. Everything the class description, protection class and converted option contributed on the building side flows into Business Income untouched.

What Business Income adds is a single factor. Not a rate, not a territory, not a construction dimension — one number representing the time-element characteristics of the risk. The chain is three steps long.

The thing most likely to be built wrong: SetFactor looks like it should multiply four factors together — Type of Risk, Maximum Period of Indemnity, Monthly Limit of Indemnity, Extra Expense. It does not. It is a four-way Choose that picks exactly one, first match wins. The other three are computed during prep and discarded. Multiplying them would overstate the premium by a large multiple — Manufacturing's Maximum Period factor alone is 1.93.

2. The rating chain, step by step

#StepWhat it does
1BasicGroupIRate Copy from ../../BasicGroupIRate — the parent Special Class item's Basic Group I rate, after its own base rate × loss cost multiplier. No lookup, no table, no keys.
2BasicGroupICauseOfLossAdjustment BasicGroupIRate × VandalismExclFactor. The vandalism exclusion factor is computed in shared prep on the Business Income group's own SetVandalismExclFactor. No cause-of-loss gate, no zeroing branch.
3Factor Set in shared prep, before the form chains run. A four-way Choose — see §3. This is the only place Business Income characteristics enter the rate.
4FinalBasicGroupIRate BasicGroupICauseOfLossAdjustment × Factor, rounded to 3 dp. Called from SetFinalRates, which fires all five forms' final-rate rules together.

Note what is absent compared with the building-side Basic Group I: no coinsurance factor in the rate (coinsurance enters as a key to the factor lookup instead), no deductible factor of any kind, no by-location factor, no LOI factor.

3. SetFactor — the four-way pick

Factor =
    MaxPeriodOfIndemnityFactor        if MaxPeriod = "Yes"
    elif MonthlyLimitOfIndemnity <> "Not Applicable":
        MonthlyLimitOfIndemnityFactor
    elif CovType = "Extra Expense Only":
        ExtraExpenseFactor
    else:
        TypeOfRiskFactor              <-- the ordinary path

First match wins, and only one factor ever reaches the rate. The ordering matters: a risk written with Maximum Period of Indemnity and a monthly limit takes the Maximum Period factor and ignores the monthly limit entirely.

BranchTableKeysCW shape
Type of Risk (default)BusnIncomeFactorState, CovType, TypeOfRisk, CoinsuranceToUse131 rows. This is where coinsurance enters — e.g. Mercantile & Non-manufacturing, BI With Extra Expense: 50% → 0.76, 80% → 0.59, 100% → 0.50.
Maximum Period of IndemnityMaxPeriodOfIndemnityFactorState, CovType, TypeOfRisk22 rows. Mercantile 1.15, Manufacturing 1.93, Mining 1.00, Rental 0.88. No coinsurance key.

Each of the first three also has a combination path — TypeRiskCombination1Factor/2, and the same for the other two — used when the risk splits across type-of-risk categories by percentage (CombinedTypeOfRiskMercantilePct, ...ManufacturingPct, ...RentalPct, ...EducationalPct). Those percentage splits, plus TuitionAndFeesFactor and ChangesEducationalInstitutionsExtensionOfRecoveryFactor, feed SetTypeOfRiskFactor — at 14,000 characters it is by far the largest rule in this chain and has not been traced branch by branch in this pass.

4. The full formula, end to end

BasicGroupIRate =
    Copy of ../../BasicGroupIRate           # the parent Special Class item's BGI rate

BasicGroupICauseOfLossAdjustment =
    BasicGroupIRate × VandalismExclFactor

Factor =
    <the four-way Choose from §3>

FinalBasicGroupIRate =
    BasicGroupICauseOfLossAdjustment × Factor        (round to 3 dp)

Premium (scheduled, IncludedInBlkt = "No") =
    round((Limit/100) × FinalBasicGroupIRate × PackageModFactor × IRPMFactor, 0)
      × CyberIncidentExclusionFactorBGI
      × CyberIncidentExclusionCOLExcptnsFactorBusnIncomeBGIToUse

Premium (blanket) =
    round((BlktLimit/100) × (FullBusnIncomeValue/BlktTotalFullValueAmount)
          × BlktBasicGroupIAvgRate × PackageModFactor × IRPMFactor, 0)
      × CyberIncidentExclusionFactorBGI
      × CyberIncidentExclusionCOLExcptnsFactorBusnIncomeBGIToUse

5. Premium — two departures from the building side

No MultiPremiumAndDispersionCreditFactor. Every building-side Special Class premium formula carries it. This one does not — confirmed by direct read of the SetPremium datadef list. A multi-location dispersion credit does not reach Business Income.

The cyber pair is asymmetric. The first factor, CyberIncidentExclusionFactorBGI, is read from the policy level (seven levels up) and is the same value the building side uses — 0.995 when CP 10 75 is attached. The second is CyberIncidentExclusionCOLExcptnsFactorBusnIncomeBGIToUse, read from the Business Income item's own parent (../) — a Business-Income-specific datadef with its own ToUse resolution, not the building-side CyberIncidentExclusionCOLExcptnsFactorBGI. Do not point these two cells at the same place.

No state overrides the premium rule.

6. State deviations — swept, not assumed

All 46 state rule files and the rate tables compared against countrywide. This chain is unusually clean.

StateWhat deviatesEffect
FLSetBasicGroupICauseOfLossAdjustment ruleSubtraction, not multiplication. Florida files BasicGroupICauseOfLossAdjustment = round(BasicGroupIRate − VandalismExclRate, 3) — reading a VandalismExclRate rather than a Factor. Structurally a different operation, not a different number. Building this as a multiplication in Florida is wrong in kind, not just in degree.
FLBusnIncomeFactor tableThe only state that files its own. Same 131 keys, 64 of them at different values — consistently higher, e.g. Mercantile & Non-manufacturing / BI With Extra Expense: 50% is 0.89 against CW's 0.76, 100% is 0.59 against CW's 0.50.
All other 45—Countrywide rules and countrywide tables throughout. No state overrides SetFactor, SetFinalBasicGroupIRate, SetTypeOfRiskFactor, SetCoinsuranceToUse or LookupBusnIncomeFactor.
Compared with the building side, this is easy. Basic Group II had six distinct state rule shapes for coinsurance alone and 46 states filing their own deductible tables. Business Income Basic Group I has one deviating state and one deviating table.

7. What a build would need

  • Four tables: BusnIncomeFactor (131 rows × 2 — CW and FL), MaxPeriodOfIndemnityFactor (22), MonthlyLimitOfIndemnityFactor (60), ExtraExpenseFactor (4). Small by this project's standards.
  • New inputs: Type of Risk, Coverage Type (BI With / Without Extra Expense / Extra Expense Only), Coinsurance, Maximum Period of Indemnity yes/no, Monthly Limit of Indemnity (1/3, 1/4, 1/6, Not Applicable), Extra Expense limits on loss payment.
  • Reuse as-is: the parent item's BasicGroupIRate — already built.
  • One Florida branch at the cause-of-loss adjustment: subtract rather than multiply.
  • Two premium cells, one of which is Business-Income-specific.
