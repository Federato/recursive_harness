CF premium map — every premium field, what feeds it, where it ends up

The question this answers: for Commercial Property, where does every premium number come from, and how does it reach the one figure that is the account's premium?

The answer in one line: CommercialProperty.ErcCalculatedTotalPremium is the account total, and every one of the 881 groups that carry a premium reaches it — through as many as seven levels of summation.

Source: CFCW20260601V01, countrywide, 882 rule files, plus the latest package of all 47 states for the jurisdiction analysis.

Navigable version: CF_Algorithm/CF_Premium_Tree.html — collapsible, searchable, with a jurisdiction selector. Companion data: CF_Algorithm/CF_Premium_Map.xlsx — Summary, RollUpTree (881 rows), PremiumSources (546 rows), PremiumFields (212 rows).

Companion for GL: GL_Algorithm/GL_Premium_Map.md. The two lines share a mechanism and almost nothing else; the differences are the point of this document.

1. The same three mechanisms, at twice the scale

Rule CF GL
SetPremium — rate into dollars on one record 546 322
SetPremiumIndicator — did this record produce a premium 546 322
CalculateTotalPremium — collect upward one level 881 571

Identical architecture. A leaf contributes its Premium only when PremiumIndicator = 1 and Premium ≠ 0; an aggregator sums its children into ErcCalculatedTotalPremium; children are reached by ForEach over an <X>Table when repeating and by Locate when singleton. Nothing about the mechanism is CF-specific.

Build, 2026-10-02 (chunk 14, docs/CF-CHUNK14-GATE.md). The engine assembles the account total exactly this way, and ISO agrees: the total adds whole-dollar premiums, negatives included (a rounding case gives $586, not $587); it sums 45 record types; the 41 state overrides of the total only add state records. ISO applies no minimum premium (MinimumPremium / PolMinPremium file 0 rows); a minimum is a company input with a "refer to company" outcome — OI-113, chunk 28 set E, not built. The policy-wide modifiers (IRPM, package modification, multi-premium credit, the Standard Property Policy's 0.98) act in the rate chain before SetPremium, not on the total.

ErcCalculatedTotalPremium is written 261 times across the corpus and read zero times — exactly as in GL. Every stored subtotal is an output for the caller, never an input to the level above.

2. Where CF and GL genuinely differ: the shape

GL is wide and shallow. CF is deep and narrow.

Depth below the account total CF groups GL groups
0 — the account 1 1
1 45 419
2 23 110
3 167 34
4 304 7
5 230 —
6 79 —
7 32 —

GL puts 419 of 571 groups directly on the policy — endorsements that price at policy level. CF puts only 45 there and pushes the mass to depths 4 and 5, because Property's premium follows the property: policy → location → building → occupancy class → contents, and endorsements attach to whichever of those they modify rather than to the policy.

The practical consequence: a CF premium can be seven summations away from the account total. Anything that walks this tree — a UI, a reconciliation, a trace — has to handle depth it will never see in GL.

4. Interim premium — and where CF differs from GL

For GL the finding was blunt: a fourteen-field pro-rata / written / earned / endorsement block sits on every record and nothing reads or writes any of it, in any of 52 jurisdictions (OI-98). CF was measured the same way, across all 48 jurisdictions, and the answer is nearly the same — with three real exceptions that are worth understanding.

Still completely inert in CF

Field Writes Reads
AnnualPremium · PolicyTermPremium · PriorPremium 0 0
ProRatedPremium · ProRatedPriorPremium · ProRatedChangePremium · TotalProRatedChangePremium 0 0
ChangePremium · EvaluatedPremium · OverridePremium 0 0
TermFactorIndicator · EarnedInFullIndicator 0 0

The policy-level administration block is as dead in CF as in GL. No mid-term endorsement pricing, no written-vs-earned split, no cancellation return arithmetic.

But three mechanisms are live

Unlike GL, CF files genuine term arithmetic — in exactly the places where a coverage is intrinsically part-term or intrinsically reported:

Field Writes / reads Where Why it exists
ProRateFactor 4 / 4 SetVacancyPermitProRateFactor on CommercialPropertyVacancyPermitPrsnlProp a Vacancy Permit covers part of a term by definition

FormAnnualPremium, AnnualPremiumOfPrecedingReportingPeriod 10 / 25, 10 / 20 CommercialPropertyBuildersRiskPremiumAdjustmentForm (CW, and FL files its own) a reporting form adjusts against the prior reporting period

Build, 2026-10-02 — all three are built and ISO-confirmed. Vacancy Permit (chunk 18): pro-rata = permit days ÷ policy days (90 → 0.247), so the payload must carry the policy's EffectiveDt / ExpirationDt; a submitted pro-rata is honoured, at ISO too. Peak Season (chunk 18): (premium at the total limit − at the base limit) × the season's pro-rata (60 days → 0.164). Builders Risk premium adjustment (chunk 24): each reporting period is re-priced and pro-rated by plain exclusive days, no leap day removed (OI-121, the one engine change in that chunk, made with the user's go); a period after expiration prices negative, as written, at ISO too. One more term-shaped mechanism the measurement above did not name: the Value Reporting Form (chunk 22) carries one average rate per policy and switches the ordinary contents premium off.

This refines OI-98 rather than contradicting it. ISO still does not administer a policy — there is no general proration engine, no transaction model, no earned/written split. What it does file is coverage-intrinsic term arithmetic: where the coverage itself is defined as partial-term or as a reporting form, the proration is part of the rating content because it is part of the rate.

The line to hold: proration belongs to ISO when it is part of what the coverage is, and to the policy system when it is a consequence of when the transaction happened. CF has the first. Neither line has the second.

5. What the state packages do — and CF is nothing like GL here

This is the largest CF/GL divergence in the whole map.

CF GL
Groups whose SetPremium some state rewrites 213 4
Total override instances 479 12
States that rewrite at least one 21 of 47 6 of 51
Premium groups existing only in state packages 223 457

GL states rewrite exactly one thing — Terrorism. CF states rewrite the rating itself, at scale.

Heaviest rewriters: FL 122, PR 64, CA 61, KS 46, NY 43, RI 40, AL 31. Florida alone rewrites more premium formulas than every GL state combined, ten times over.

The most contested formulas:

Group States

…OutdoorTreesShrubsAndPlantsBasicGroupIICoverage 7 — AR DC FL KS NY PR RI

CommercialPropertyPostTRIACoverage 6 — CA DC IL MD NY VA

What this does not mean. It is not a finding that CF needs state-over-countrywide resolution. That was measured long ago — 100.0% of 23,404 Overridden rules shadow a same-named parent — it is non-negotiable N5, and cf_engine/resolve/resolver.py already implements it. Any correctly built engine composes the layers regardless of how many rules sit in each.

What it does mean is a testing number. It says how much a single-state proof is worth. A GL coverage proven in one state is very nearly proven everywhere — only Terrorism varies. A CF coverage proven in one state proves much less, because 213 formulas differ somewhere. That is a claim about where to spend state test cases, not about architecture: start with the contested formulas above, because those are where a countrywide-derived expectation is most likely to be wrong.

Neither line ever deletes a premium rule. Zero empty-body premium overrides in CF, as in GL — the opposite of CF's validation messages, where 316 countrywide rules are switched off by an empty state override. Premium rules are added to or replaced, never silently removed.

Build, 2026-10-02 — true of SetPremium, but a state can still switch a premium off. Chunk 15 found states zeroing whole families upstream of SetPremium: GA, TX and VA file an empty ErcRate for the manual-premium endorsements and FL forces $0; MO's countrywide parent (CW 2023 V02) has no Discretionary Payroll premium rule at all. Chunk 23 found AK 2020 and VA 2021 pricing inside ErcRate (old packages only). A SetPremium-only census cannot see either. Every chunk since 3 has run its family in all 46 states against ISO (Puerto Rico is counted in this map but excluded from the build by standing rule), and four countrywide parents are in force (2026 V01 for 37 states; 2023 V04, V03, V02), so a state's formula can differ by edition as well as by override. The six states rewriting CommercialPropertyPostTRIACoverage (CA DC IL MD NY VA) are exactly the six with the 25% terrorism cap confirmed in chunk 13.

6. Reading this for a specific premium

  1. Find the group in RollUpTree — Path to account total is the full chain of summations between that record and the account figure. Expect up to seven links.
  2. Check the state first. Unlike GL, the odds that a CF premium formula is state-rewritten are material: 213 groups, 21 states. The tree page's jurisdiction selector flags it.
  3. If it is a leaf, its Premium came from SetPremium — PremiumSources lists the fields that rule reads.
  4. If a premium is missing rather than wrong, check PremiumIndicator before the arithmetic. A coverage that rated to zero and a coverage that was never on the policy both contribute 0; the indicator is the only discriminator.

7. Limits of this map

  • The roll-up tree is countrywide. State overrides of SetPremium are counted and attributed, but the tree shape is CW's. A state adding a premium-bearing group adds a node this tree does not draw; the jurisdiction panel lists them instead.
  • Structural, not evaluated — the map itself. It is read from rule XML. Build, 2026-10-02: the evaluation now exists separately: every group is rated by the engine and each chunk's premiums are ISO-confirmed (gate files docs/CF-CHUNK1–27-GATE.md; replay 157_ --all, snapshot 206_ --check).
  • The jurisdiction overlay uses each state's latest-dated package in the corpus. For some states that is later than the package ISO serves today; the engine and ISO agree on the package in all 46 states at 2026-09-17 (OI-105, chunk 28 set F).
  • SetPremium inputs are flattened from nested conditions — a rule reading eight fields may need only two in any given branch.
  • Rate resolution is out of scope, as in GL. This map covers rate-to-dollars and the aggregation above it; how a rate is built is the rating chain.
