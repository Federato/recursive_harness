Twenty-two chains, one shape, a lot of exceptions

Building, Personal Property, and Special Class each run four cause-of-loss chains — Basic Group I, Basic Group II, Broad, Special — that compute a rate unconditionally, then let a coverage gate decide which one actually produces premium. The two Business Income coverages run those same four plus a real fifth chain, Earthquake — though Earthquake only ever produces premium through its Agreed Value coverage record. Special Class Business Income is the tightest-coupled of the five coverages: two of its five forms don't rate anything at all — they borrow the coinsured Special Class item's already-finished rate wholesale. Click any cell for its full decision tree: every gate, every branch, every terminal, cited back to the source rule.

Dashed amber-and-rust nodes mark a flagged finding — an empty rate table, an unresolved reference, a hard-coded value — logged in BUILD-LOG.md rather than guessed at.

What to notice

  • Rating type forks the whole chain up front — a class-rated risk and a specific/tentative-rated risk barely share any downstream math.
  • Legal Liability premium bypasses coinsurance, LOI, and deductible entirely — it multiplies straight off the cause-of-loss-adjusted rate.
  • Coinsurance has its own skip condition (class 1150, or Functional Valuation) that overrides the normal percentage branch.

What to notice

  • Territory is a lookup key here, not a multiplier the way it is in Basic Group I — a real difference in how the two forms use the same concept.

  • The premium file is a byte-identical copy of Basic Group I's, confirmed by diff — every branch, gate, and rounding rule carries over unchanged.
