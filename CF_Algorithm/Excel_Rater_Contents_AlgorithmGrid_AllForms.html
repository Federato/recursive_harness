The one constant across all four

Two things never vary, form to form, for Contents specifically.

✓No roof-surfacing factor, ever, anywhere. Every one of the four forms' Contents chains explicitly excludes it — confirmed in each source doc, not just an absence. Buildings have roofs; the property inside them doesn't need a roof-surfacing limitation.
✓No multi-residential credit multiplier, ever, anywhere. FinalRate's shape is identical across all four forms for this reason alone — 3-term product, no fourth multiplicand. Only AdjustedRate upstream of it actually varies in term count (Broad has 3, Special has 5, BGI/BGII have 3).
⚠Broad is the real outlier, not just "another form." It's the only one of the four with no COLAdj stage at all — AdjustedRate reads raw Rate directly — and the only one that computes LOIFactor before BaseRate in the source doc's own step order. Neither changes the math, but both matter if you're translating the doc's step numbering directly into calc-tab column order.
⚠Special's Premium step doesn't round the same way the other three do. BGI, BGII, and Broad all wrap the whole IRPM × ... × FinalRate × (Limit/100) product in a single ROUND(..., 0), then multiply the Cyber factors in afterward. Special uses a nested Product with DecimalPlaces=0 applied at every intermediate step instead — no single outer ROUND() at all. On most inputs this lands on the same whole-dollar premium either way, but the two approaches aren't guaranteed identical, and a naive copy-paste of BGI's premium formula onto Special would silently get the rounding wrong.
✓Special is also the only form with a Limit override at the premium step. PP_LimitToUse substitutes PP_AlcoholicBeveragesTaxExclusionLimit for the plain PP_Limit whenever that endorsement (CP 12 11) is elected — a Contents-only sublimit override with no Building equivalent and no counterpart in BGI, BGII, or Broad.
