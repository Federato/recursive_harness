Category by category

1 Building — Basic Group I, II, Broad, Special Near parity

Closest to parity. All four forms are built in the rater (Bldg Rating Worksheet rows 2–99) and all four are gated in the harness (chunks 1–3). What the rater is missing is the edges the harness proved: Florida and Puerto Rico rate Special through their own overriding rule and a differently-named table (PR keys on construction type and uses a cause-of-loss domain that does not exist countrywide) — the rater has no such branch. The harness also catalogued eight "absent input prices at zero in silence" cases, including a correctly-spelled ConstructionType like Joisted Masonry that zeroes the whole Broad chain; the rater has no equivalent guard cells. Rounding is settled in the harness as ROUND_HALF_UP, proven over 58,656 combinations and two ISO tie-break calls; the rater has never had its rounding tested. Specific/Tentative rating is absent from both, so that is a shared gap, not a rater gap.

Corrected 2026-09-22: Specific/Tentative is built in the rater — Location Inputs!B28 offers Specific, and B44/B46/B48 carry dedicated Specific/Tentative rate inputs for Building, Contents and Business Income. It remains absent from the harness, so the direction of this gap is reversed: here the rater is ahead.

2 Contents (Personal Property) Partial

The harness rates all four forms ($223 / $24 / $16 / $137, ISO-confirmed) across 46 of 47 jurisdictions, and chunk 4 was the first to rate a repeating group — N occupancy classes per location, iterated and summed the way ISO sums them. The rater has Basic Group I, II and Broad built; Contents Special has never been confirmed built (it is "assumed from sheet structure," unverified as of 2026-09-11). More consequentially, nothing in the rater's records shows the multi-occupancy-class iteration — a Contents Rating Worksheet with fixed rows cannot reproduce a location carrying several occupancy classes the way the harness does. The harness also carries the Group A/B/C/YARD two-key mechanism (building class code gates eligibility, a separate tenant-level code picks the group) and ISO's refusal of four Contents payloads missing WindstormOrHailDeductible — the rater prices those happily, with no message.

3 Business Income Parity

Roughly at parity on the core, and this is the one category where the rater is not materially behind. Both sides have Basic Group I, II, Broad and Special; the rater's four are built in BI Rating Worksheet rows 2–75, with a real Cyber Incident Exclusion premium bug found and fixed on 2026-09-11. The harness (chunk 5) adds the proof layer rather than new coverage: four forms at $112 / $13 / $23 / $47, ISO-confirmed, plus the finding that three of five forms are arithmetically downstream of Building — they read Building's already-computed rate rather than their own. Everything else is missing from both: Earthquake's plain rate chain, Agreed Value / Extended Period of Indemnity's incremental (Factor − 1) × Rate × Limit surcharge shape, and ~34 optional endorsements. The one rater-specific risk is the shared Factor (MaxPeriodOfIndemnity / MonthlyLimit / ExtraExpense / TypeOfRisk) zero-out case logged as Z-3 — filed ISO behaviour the rater is meant to reproduce deliberately, with a visible check cell, and does not yet.

4 Special Class — building side Near parity — corrected

A real gap. The harness closed chunk 6 — all four forms rating at $66 / $209 / $14 / $44 across 47 of 47 states, with the free-text class description driving two lookups in series, and Special Class correctly hung off the Location rather than inside a building record. The rater has the design work (four HowItWorks pages, an input wireframe, the Item ID scheme) and live Special Class Inputs / Special Class Worksheet tabs that were started on 2026-09-14 — but Broad and Special were explicitly left "not built into the live workbook until you say go," and how far the live tabs have moved since is unknown here. Two state traps the harness handles and the rater's tables do not: NY genuinely deviates on BasicGroupIRateSpecialClass (84 differing values, swings over 400%), and 11 of Florida's SpecialClassDescription strings do not exist in the countrywide list at all — a rater keying that table off countrywide descriptions silently misses in FL.

Corrected 2026-09-22: Broad and Special are built — Special Class rates $1,434 at Policy Inputs!I6, off a populated Special Class Inputs tab (3 item columns, 40 fields). The coverage is at near parity; what survives is the NY and FL data traps above, which are about table content, not about the chain being absent.

5 Special Class Business Income Near parity — corrected

The largest clean gap. The harness closed chunk 7 — four forms rating at $39 / $123 / $23 / $47 across 47 of 47 states, the deepest nesting in the project, and the first coverage that borrows a fully rated rate: eleven values cross the coverage boundary and not one of them is a base rate. In the rater this is documentation only — a reuse map with COPY / SHARE THE TABLE / BUILD OWN verdicts per value, a BGII walkthrough, and vandalism formulas. Nothing is in the workbook. Three traps are already written down and waiting: Broad reads the same BroadFormBaseRate table as the building side but passes a different CovType, so pointing one at the other is 36% high on one and 35% low on the other; Special reads entirely different tables despite identical rule and datadef names (0.88 building vs 0.86 Business Income); and the vandalism, wind/hail and theft exclusions each file two separate endorsement records, which the rater's single shared Yes/No input cannot express (backlog S-1 / S-2 / S-3).

Corrected 2026-09-22: "Nothing is in the workbook" was wrong. SC Business Income rates $406 at Policy Inputs!I7, with its own input block on Special Class Inputs (B37–B46: coverage type, type of risk, both percentages, limit, and all three indemnity inputs). The three rate traps listed above are still worth checking against the built formulas — they are why this is "near parity" and not "parity".

6 Legal Liability Near parity — corrected

Complete gap in the rater. The harness closed chunk 8 with Building at $60 / $7 / $4 / $12 and Contents at $132 / $15 / $11 / $93, 47 of 47 states. In the rater, Legal Liability is formula-only — written down, never built into calc columns. The mechanism the harness implements and the rater does not: the fork is in the premium, not the rate chain — the Legal Liability branch picks the rate up three steps earlier than the Class-rated branch, so coinsurance, the LOI factor and the deductible factors are computed and then ignored, and MultiPremiumAndDispersionCreditFactor is never applied. Building and Contents do not share the mechanism, only the table: Building gates on CovType, Contents on CovForm inherited from the occupancy class, so Legal Liability and ordinary contents cannot be mixed inside one occupancy class. Also unbuilt in the rater: AddlInsurableIntOption as a mandatory four-option input — blank drives the factor to 0.0 and the premium to zero (backlog Z-1).

Corrected 2026-09-22: Legal Liability is built. It is a selectable Building Coverage Type (Location Inputs!B8, live in column C) with the matching Legal Liability Coverage Form on the Contents side, and the additional-insurable-interest input is wired — B92 holds Option 1 with its decoded description at B93. The Z-1 zero-premium guard is the part still worth verifying; the chain itself is not missing.

7 Blanket rating Widest gap

The single widest gap, and the one that touches every other category. The harness closed chunk 9 at 9 of 9 — the only chunk to date that passes every gate point — with a golden case of $255 / $510, hand-derived, exact, and confirmed live against ISO to the dollar with all three validation messages included. It also overturned the premise: no premium file stubs blanket out; all 20 carry the complete filed formula, and the missing piece was an interpreter bug in ForEach AtInputDataDef that reparented writes onto the iterated record. In the rater, blanket is unbuilt line-wide. Every coverage's Branch B is dead, which blocks FullSpecialClassValue, BlktCoinsurance and BlktLimit everywhere — not just on Special Class. Related and also unbuilt: the harness confirmed MultiPremiumAndDispersionCreditFactor is live on 12 of 20 premium files and genuinely absent on the other 8, and that IRPM/PackageMod level counts vary by group (5/7/9, not uniformly 5). The rater carries independent flat 1.0 rows per tab — flagged in Entry 33, never fixed.

8 Earthquake Missing in both

Near-parity at zero, with the harness slightly ahead on the map. Chunk 10 opened 2026-09-22 with a census and 21 open tasks; no engine code, nothing run. But the census already corrects the shape: the 97 *Earthquake*-named rule files are not the rating engine — the real chain (SetEQBaseRate, SetEQRate, SetFinalEQRate, SetEQCauseOfLossAdjustment) lives inside the same five parent files chunks 1–9 already opened, and those 97 files run a wholly separate SetPremium that adds its own premium node. Two premium trees on one submission — the opposite shape from blanket. Earthquake shares zero multipliers with the ordinary chain and runs on 57 distinct EQ-prefixed deductible/coinsurance names. EQClass and EQTerr are pure submission inputs, never derived. The rater has none of this: Earthquake is untraced and unbuilt for every coverage, and it gates ~29 Special Class rule files that each ship an Earthquake wrapper. The harness also has a ready regression set — 69 of ISO's 924 STC payloads elect Earthquake across at least 8 states.

9 State and edition resolution Structural gap

Structural, not per-coverage. The harness has a resolver that walks countrywide base → state child → edition chain at rate time, and every chunk enumerates 46–47 of 47 jurisdictions with a premium predicted before the run, not sampled. The rater resolves state and edition outside the workbook: rates were pulled into Supporting Tables workbooks with the edition picked once by a 9/1/2026 cutoff, and the workbook itself has no edition-transition logic — Policy.EffectiveDate exists as a column with a note that this pass assumes one edition. Practical consequences: a filing that takes effect after the pull date silently does not apply; the AK / CA / NY District-split states are in the rater as known-inaccurate shortcuts (NY a built shortcut, AK a ZIP mapping), not production-ready; and HI, ID, LA, MS, WA are structurally absent from the corpus in both. The harness also found a real ISO filed-data defect — LowestBasicGroupIIRate symbol A PersProp in CF AL 20270301 V01 is 0.8 where eight prior editions file 0.08, a 10× error that hits the wind/hail-excluded Contents branch.

10 Proof and verification Deepest gap

This is the deepest difference, and it is not a coverage at all. The harness gates every chunk against nine points: a golden case hand-derived before code with every intermediate traced to a named rule and table row; every table's row count stated; every state enumerated rather than sampled; every 0 tested against its consuming branch; every hardcoded literal listed; shared-name traps diffed (83 across the corpus once RunRule delegation was followed); every exclusion's endorsement record identified per datadef group; the edition basis stated; and no unexplained validation message. Points 1–7 hold on all eight closed chunks, and premiums have been confirmed against live ISO RAaS calls repeatedly — seven calls in chunk 3 alone, with message counts matching exactly in all seven. The rater has none of this scaffolding: no regression suite, no ISO call comparison, no zero-case catalogue, no message register. Its verification is read-only formula review plus spot checks — which is how three genuine bugs were caught, but it is a per-session activity, not a standing gate. Point 9 (validation messages) is owed on harness chunks 1–8 too.
