R-8 Phase 0 — making the workbook hold ISO's hierarchy

2026-09-22. Spec only — nothing has been built. You chose the real structure over the cheap "fake items sharing a building" workaround. This says what gets built, in what order, and what each step has to prove before the next one starts.

The decision behind this. Multiple contents types on one building — Stock, Machinery, Tenants Improvements — cannot be expressed today. Faking it with separate items fragments CoreScratchpad's TOTAL LIMIT, which drives the banded deductible factor, and double-counts the building's own limit. It would price simple risks right and complex ones wrong, silently. 16 Personal Property coverage types exist in ISO's domain; the workbook holds one per building.

Why one sheet, and why this shape: it is exactly the Payload Export layout already specified for the generator. Building the input model in that shape means Phase 6 deletes the export sheet rather than rewriting it — the inputs become the payload structure. Two jobs, one build.

3. Decision two — the calc sheets keep their orientation

The tempting move is to flip the calc sheets to one row per record. I am recommending against it.

Today every CALC - sheet is rows = rating steps, columns = items. The idiomatic Excel answer for N records is to flip that — records down, steps across. But:

  • R-3's Source and Check columns assume rows = steps. 434 annotated rows and 174 live checks, built today, would all need rebuilding.
  • R-7's diagnostics roll-up reads those columns. It would follow.
  • Every CoreScratchpad block is built that way — it is the shared machinery behind all four coverages.

Recommendation: keep rows = steps and add columns. What changes is only what a column means: today it is "location + building + occupancy + contents" fused together; afterwards it is "one record of this coverage". Different sheets get different column counts — CALC - Bldg Rating one per building, CALC - Contents Rating one per contents record.

Formulas fill right instead of down. Excel allows 16,384 columns; provisioning 40 records per coverage costs nothing and preserves R-3, R-7 and CoreScratchpad intact. The cost is honest: wide sheets are less pleasant to read than tall ones. That is the trade, and it is worth it to avoid rebuilding three days of work.

4. The mechanism that actually fixes your question

The deductible factor is keyed on CoreScratchpad!B138 TOTAL LIMIT, today a flat Building + Contents for one fused item. It becomes a roll-up over every contents record sharing a building:

TOTAL LIMIT (per building)
  = building's own Limit
  + SUMIFS( Contents.Limit , Contents.ParentOcc , occupancy classes under this building )

So a building with $300k Stock and $215k Machinery still sees $1,190,000 — one correct deductible band — while each contents record carries its own rate. That single formula is the difference between the real structure and the cheap one.

5. Phases, and what each must prove

1 New model alongside the old ~½ day Gate: nothing reads it, premiums provably untouched

Build IN - Schedule with all six blocks, populated from the current risk so the two models describe the same policy. Nothing is wired. This is deliberately inert.

2 Migrate Contents only ~1 day Gate: Contents premium still 8,022

Repoint CALC - Contents Rating at the new blocks; widen it to one column per contents record; rebuild the TOTAL LIMIT roll-up. Contents is chosen first because it is where the 16 CovTypes live — it is the coverage the whole exercise is for.

3 Prove multi-record ~½ day Gate: ISO agrees, via RAaS

Add a second contents type and rate it. Verified against ISO, not against ourselves — an internally consistent answer proves nothing here, because the whole question is whether ISO sums a repeating group the way we do. Harness chunk 4 says it does; this confirms it in the workbook.

This is the phase that decides the project. If ISO disagrees, phases 4–6 do not happen and we have spent two days, not six.

4 Building, BI, Special Class, SCBI ~2–3 days Gate: each premium unchanged, one coverage at a time

Same pattern, one coverage per step, premium diffed after each. No coverage starts until the previous one's gate passes.

5 Retire the old inputs ~½ day Gate: all five premiums unchanged

IN - Location and IN - Special Class become read-only remnants, then are removed once nothing references them. Not deleted earlier — they are the fallback if a gate fails mid-migration.

6 Collapse the payload export ~½ day

The export sheet exists to transpose column-per-item into row-per-record. After Phase 1 the inputs are row-per-record, so most blocks become direct references and the sheet largely disappears. Payload-generator Step 4 then becomes trivial.
