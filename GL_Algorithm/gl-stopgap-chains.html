Employers liability priced as a small factor on Premises/Operations' own base rate

In five jurisdictions the workers' compensation fund is monopolistic and leaves out employers liability, so GL fills the gap by endorsement. Stop Gap has no ISO subline code and no countrywide rating package. Each state's classification ErcRate override runs the countrywide rate chain first, then four Stop Gap rules. Those rules multiply Premises/Operations' computed base rate by a two-value factor and the Premises ILF at Stop Gap's own limits, and apply it to Premises' computed exposure.

The rule file is identical in every edition of all five states. The edition matters through data: the limit domain and classification labels change at 2027, and so does the Premises base rate underneath. ND, OH, PR and WY move to 2027 editions on 2027-04-01. WA has no 2027 package and keeps its pre-2027 one.

Stop Gap factor
.03 / .02

Manufacturing and contracting classes .03, everything else .02. The same in all five states, with no countrywide row to fall back on.

Equal limits required
Accident = Employee

When the each-accident and disease each-employee limits differ, the manual says refer. ERC prices a supplied ManualPremium, or $0 without one.

2027 limit spelling
"BI" dropped

"500,000 BI" becomes "500,000". The other edition's spelling misses the ILF and Stop Gap prices $0, in either direction.

What to notice

  • Stop Gap has no exposure base and no base rate of its own. It reads both from the Premises group after Premises has rated (E18), so it moves whenever the Premises base rate moves.
  • The final rate is rounded to 3 decimals before exposure. Stop Gap rates are only a few thousandths, so that rounding alone can move the premium by several percent.
  • The modifiers are PackageMod, Experience and ModToUse, read from the policy record. There is no Expense modifier.
No referral fires. The engine's register has no entry for Stop Gap. A limit mismatch with no ManualPremium, a wrong limit spelling and a record without StopGap = "Yes" all price $0 silently, in underwriting mode too.

What to notice

  • The limit-equality test happens three times: in the ILF and factor guards, in the class premium, and again at policy level.
  • The ILF is the Premises table with Stop Gap's own limits standing in for Premises' occurrence and aggregate limits (manual B.1.d, Rule 23.D).
  • An ILF override field on the classification replaces the looked-up ILF when it is non-zero. No rule writes it, so it is a carrier input.
Silent $0 paths. A record with StopGap not "Yes", unequal limits with no ManualPremium, and a limit spelled the 2027 way all price Stop Gap at $0. None raises a message or a referral.

What to notice

  • The limit spelling is the whole edition difference that a submission controls. The engine's variant builder renormalizes Premises and Products limits, but not these three Stop Gap fields.
  • A 2027 submission is not portable to WA, and a WA submission is not portable to the other four. WA expects " BI" at every date.
  • Premium can still move at 2027 with no Stop Gap rule changing, because the Premises base rate and ILF under it are refiled.
Spelling trap, both ways. "500,000 BI" on a 2027 ND edition, or "500,000" on WA or a pre-2027 edition, misses the ILF row and prices Stop Gap at $0, silently.

Five differences, none of them in Stop Gap's own rules

The policy-level rule file is identical in every edition of all five states. The four classification rules are the same in every state override. What differs is data and the Premises host.
