A worked scenario — how six coverages become one policy premium

The risk. One location, one classification (Mercantile), written on the Premises/Operations and Products/Completed Operations subline, with:

  • Premises/Operations coverage
  • Products/Completed Operations coverage
  • Liquor Liability
  • Medical Payments at $10,000
  • Terrorism (TRIA) accepted
  • Minimum premiums in play

Source: GL CW 20270401 V01 (V02 identical for everything here). Companions: GL_Premium_Map.md (the whole line), GL_PremOps_Prods_Premium_Trace.md (the two core coverages in detail), GL_Premium_Tree.html (navigable).

Written: 2026-09-17.

Reconciled against the built engine: 2026-10-02 (commit 630ba2f) — the scenario was built and rated offline (strict-erc): Oklahoma, class 10026 Antique Stores (Mercantile), $1,000,000 gross sales on both coverages, 1,000,000 CSL, Med Pay $10,000, TerrorismCoverage = Yes, at 2027-04-01 (GL_OK_20270401_V04 over GL_CW_20270401_V02) and at 2026-10-02 (GL_OK_20261001_V01 over GL_CW_20261001_V01). The run corrected four claims this page used to make — Liquor on the same risk, the terrorism formula, a declined-terrorism zero, and the caller-supplied minimum top-up — each marked Corrected 2026-10-02 below. All 16 tests/verify_* suites pass.

Edition scope. The mechanics below are the 2027-04-01 edition. A policy effective before that date (today: CW 20260101 V01 for 46 jurisdictions, 20261001 V01 for OK, 20231201 V03 for NJ/TX/VT, 20231201 V02 for CA — and GA, NJ, NY, WA, CA even after the cliff) prices Med Pay and minimum premium differently; §7 gives the same scenario rated on the in-force edition.

1. First correction: that is five premium-bearing things, not six

Medical Payments does not produce a premium. There is a GeneralLiabilityMedPayCoverage record, and it does sit in the roll-up — but its rule file contains only InitializeRuleSet, CalculateTotalPremium and ErcProcess. No SetPremium, no rating logic of any kind. The one thing that ever writes its Premium is a line in ErcProcess that forces it to 0.0 when the coverage is off.

Medical Payments is priced as a factor inside the Premises/Operations rate:

if MedPayCoverage/Limit = "5,000":            MedicalPaymentsFactor = 1.0     (the included limit)
otherwise:                                     MedicalPaymentsFactor = LookupIncreasedMedPayLimitFactor(
                                                    State|CW, ClassificationType, MedPayLimit )

and then, in SetFinalILF on the Prem/Ops coverage:

FinalILF = round( (CSLILF + MedicalPaymentsFactor − 1) − FinalDeductibleFactor , 3 )

(skipped — plain CSLILF − FinalDeductibleFactor — when a Coverage C Medical Payments exclusion is attached at the classification or location).

At $10,000, Mercantile, the countrywide factor is 1.007 — so Med Pay adds 0.007 to the ILF. Engine: CSLILF 2.01 → FinalILF 2.017, FinalRate 0.410 × 2.017 = 0.827, Prem/Ops premium 827 against 824 at $5,000 — $3, about 0.4%. Contracting or Servicing pays 1.011 for the same limit.

Corrected 2026-10-02 — the full table (CW 20270401 V01 and V02, IncreasedMedPayLimitFactor, 20 rows; the earlier version showed a dash for Contracting at $20,000 and stopped there):

Increased Med Pay limit Mercantile, Miscellaneous, Manufacturing or Processing, Buildings or Premises Contracting or Servicing
10,000 1.007 1.011
15,000 1.011 1.017
20,000 1.014 1.022
25,000 1.016 1.026

Consequences worth knowing. Med Pay never appears as a line item. It touches Premises/Operations only — not Products, not Liquor (engine: Products FinalILF stays 1.760). And because it lands inside FinalILF, it is inside the terrorism base too (§3).

2. The premiums, and where each is computed

# Coverage DataDef group Depth Computed by
1 Premises/Operations GeneralLiabilityClassificationPremOpsCoverage 3 SetPremium
2 Products/Completed Ops GeneralLiabilityClassificationProdsCompldOpsCoverage 3 SetPremium
3 Liquor Liability GeneralLiabilityClassificationLiquorCoverage 3 SetPremium — on a separate risk, see below
4 Terrorism — Prem/Ops GeneralLiabilityTerrorismPremOpsCoverage 4 SetPremium
5 Terrorism — Prods/CompldOps GeneralLiabilityTerrorismProdsCompldOpsCoverage 4 SetPremium
(5b) Terrorism — all other sublines GeneralLiabilityTerrorismAllOtherSublineCoverage 4 SetPremium — this is where Liquor's terrorism charge lands

Corrected 2026-10-02 — Liquor cannot sit on this risk. Every coverage on a classification is switched on by the policy's Subline. GeneralLiabilityClassificationLiquorCoverage.SetCoverageOnPolicyIndicator is 1 only when Subline = "Liquor"; Prem/Ops is 1 only for Premises/Operations and Products/Completed Operations. One GeneralLiability risk has one subline, so a liquor record added to this risk is gated off: rated by the engine, it contributes 0 and the total stays 852. Liquor is written as a second GeneralLiability risk in the same submission (Subline = "Liquor", its own liquor class, exposure and LiquorCoverageForm); the kernel adds the risks' totals (engine: a two-risk submission rates to exactly the sum of its risks). The policy premium for this scenario is therefore this risk's total plus the liquor risk's total.

Coverages 1–3 are siblings under their classification. The terrorism coverages sit one level deeper, under a GeneralLiabilityTerrorism record that is itself a child of the classification.

1–3 share one shape

BaseRate  = round( LossCost × LCM , 3 )        Prem/Ops, Products; ELP × LCM when the loss cost is 0
          = round( ELP × LCM , 3 )             Liquor — always the ELP path (LiquorLossCost is header-only in CW)
FinalRate = round( BaseRate × FinalILF × SizeOfRisk × PackageMod
                     × ExperienceMod × ExpenseMod × ModToUse , 3 )
Premium   = round( FinalRate × Exposure / 1000 , 0 )

Only the field names differ — PremOpsCovExposure, ProdsCompldOpsCovExposure, LiquorExposure. Size-of-risk applies only when SizeOfRiskRatingApplies = Yes; Liquor has no size-of-risk term, and divides by 1,000 only for its gross-sales bases. Engine: Prem/Ops 0.41 → 0.410 → 0.827 → 827; Products 0.012 → 0.012 → 0.021 → 21.

The $1 floor. If the arithmetic gives 0 while exposure is above 0, Premium is forced to 1.0. Zero is reserved for "not covered". New in the 2027-04-01 edition.

3. Terrorism is a percentage of the other premiums

This is the part that makes the scenario order-dependent.

SetClassCoveragePremium on the terrorism coverage sums the premiums of the underlying coverages for that subline — Prem/Ops, Loss of Electronic Data, Cyber Incident Liability and the rest — and SetPremium then picks one of three formulas:

Corrected 2026-10-02 — the earlier single formula multiplied every factor together; the rule does not:

TerrorismCoverage = "Yes":
    Premium = round( ClassCoveragePremium × CertifiedActsOfTerrorismExposureClassFactor , 0 )
an NBCR exclusion endorsement attached:
    Premium = round( ClassCoveragePremium × ExposureClassFactor × NuclBioChemRadioFactor , 0 )
a terrorism aggregate-cap / sublimit endorsement attached (e.g. CG 21 80):
    Premium = round( round( ClassCoveragePremium × ExposureClassFactor , 0 )
                     × round( TerrorismILF ÷ Prem/Ops FinalILF , 3 ) , 0 )
otherwise 0;  then the $1 floor if that is 0 while ClassCoveragePremium > 0

Engine: Prem/Ops 827 × 0.004 = 3.308 → 3; Products 21 × 0.004 = 0.084 → 0 → 1 (floor). TerrorismILF is written 1.0 and the NBCR factor 0.58 is looked up, but neither is used on the accepted branch.

So terrorism cannot be computed until coverages 1–3 are final. It is a charge on the premium, not a parallel one. Three consequences:

  1. The Med Pay uplift is inside the terrorism base. A higher Med Pay limit raises Prem/Ops, which raises ClassCoveragePremium, which can raise the terrorism charge (here 827 vs 824 both round to 3). Under a cap endorsement the effect partly cancels, because the Prem/Ops FinalILF is the divisor.
  2. Liquor's terrorism goes to a different record — …TerrorismAllOtherSublineCoverage on the liquor risk, not the Prem/Ops one.
  3. Corrected 2026-10-02: declining TerrorismCoverage does not by itself zero the charge. The NBCR and cap branches do not test TerrorismCoverage. Oklahoma's engine base submission declines terrorism but carries a CG 21 80 cap at $100,000, and the engine — like ISO's own Oklahoma output — prices terrorism at 18 (2 + 16) on it. Accepting without attaching a Certified Acts form also raises ISO's message "A Certified Acts of Terrorism Form must be attached to the policy when coverage is accepted" — the premium is still produced.

4. Minimum premium — switched off in this edition

Each of the three coverages owns a minimum-premium lookup — LookupPremOpsMinPremium, LookupProdsCompldOpsMinPremium, LookupLiquorMinPremium, plus Prem/Ops's LookupSpecialCombinedMinPremium. And there are three dedicated records at policy level:

  • GeneralLiabilityPremOpsPremiumToReachMinCoverage
  • GeneralLiabilityProdsCompldOpsPremiumToReachMinCoverage
  • GeneralLiabilityLiquorPremiumToReachMinCoverage

Each is a leaf at depth 1 that is summed into the account total. But nothing computes what goes in them. Their rule files hold only InitializeRuleSet, CalculateTotalPremium, SetCoverageOnPolicyIndicator and ErcProcess — no SetPremium — and the related datadefs PremiumToReachMin and TotPremiumPriorToMin are, corpus-wide, written 0 times and read 0 times.

Corrected 2026-10-02 — and you cannot fill them either. This page used to say the caller could put a shortfall in these records and the roll-up would add it. It will not: SetCoverageOnPolicyIndicator on all three writes 0 unconditionally, so ErcProcess forces Premium = 0 whatever was sent. Engine test: Premium = 100 supplied on the Prem/Ops record, total unchanged at 852. In the 2027-04-01 edition a minimum premium has to be applied entirely outside ISO's rating** — compare and top up after the engine returns. The coverage-level lookups are not even invoked by the engine's 2027 runs.

5. How it all comes together

                                   policy premium  =  risk 1 total  +  risk 2 total
                                                         ▲                  ▲
                                   GeneralLiability (Prem/Ops & Products)   GeneralLiability (Liquor)
                                   .ErcCalculatedTotalPremium = 852         .ErcCalculatedTotalPremium
                                                         ▲                  (Liquor Premium + its
        ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────┐        All-Other terrorism)
        │                                                │          │
  PremOpsPremiumToReachMin    0      GeneralLiabilityLocation       (400+ other policy-level
  ProdsCompldOpsPremiumToReachMin 0  .ErcCalculatedTotalPremium 852  endorsements, 0 here)
  — depth 1, forced to 0 —                    ▲  depth 1
                                   GeneralLiabilityClassification
                                      .ErcCalculatedTotalPremium 852
                                               ▲  depth 2
        ┌──────────────────┬───────────────────┴──────────────┐
        │                  │                                  │
   Prem/Ops 827      Products 21                 GeneralLiabilityTerrorism 4
   depth 3           depth 3                                  ▲  depth 3
   ▲                                               ┌──────────┴──────────┐
   │                                          Terr PremOps 3       Terr Prods 1
   └── Med Pay $10,000 enters here,           depth 4              depth 4 ($1 floor)
       as +0.007 on FinalILF                  (each = ClassCoveragePremium × 0.004)

Order of computation:

  1. Rate and price Prem/Ops and Products — Med Pay's 1.007 is inside Prem/Ops's ILF.
  2. Sum those into ClassCoveragePremium per subline; compute the terrorism charges.
  3. Roll the terrorism record up into the classification alongside 1–2.
  4. Classification → location → risk.
  5. Rate the Liquor risk the same way; the submission total is the sum of the risks.
  6. Separately, outside ISO's rating, compare each subline's premium against its minimum — the …PremiumToReachMinCoverage records cannot carry the top-up in this edition.

Every contribution passes the same gate: a coverage's premium counts only when PremiumIndicator = 1 and Premium ≠ 0. A coverage priced at zero and a coverage never bought are indistinguishable in the total.

6. What is computed, and what you must supply

Computed by ISO's rules Supplied by the caller
Prem/Ops, Products, Liquor premium ✔ exposures, class code, limits, deductible — and a separate risk for Liquor
Med Pay charge ✔ (as an ILF factor) the Med Pay limit
Terrorism premium ✔ TerrorismCoverage acceptance, or the cap / NBCR endorsement
Package / experience / expense mods — all four, at policy level
Loss cost multiplier — the carrier's LCM (the CW tables hold 1)
Minimum premium top-up nothing in 2027 (computed in earlier editions — §7) the minimum, the comparison and the top-up, all outside ISO's roll-up

7. The same scenario on the edition in force today

Rated at 2026-10-02 (GL_OK_20261001_V01 over GL_CW_20261001_V01), the identical submission gives 939, not 852:

2026-10-02 2027-04-01
Prem/Ops loss cost → BaseRate 0.45 → 0.450 0.41 → 0.410
Med Pay MedPayFactor 1.02 for class 10026 (per class, not per type) → separate MedicalPaymentsCharge 9; FinalILF 2.010 factor 1.007 inside FinalILF 2.017
Prem/Ops Premium 0.905 × 1,000 + 9 = 914 827
Products Premium 21 21
Terrorism 4 + 0 (no $1 floor) 3 + 1
Minimum-premium records compute: CoveragePremium 914 / 21 / Special Combined 935, MinPremium 0, top-up 0 switched off
Total 939 852

Before 2027-04-01 ISO's rules do the minimum-premium work themselves: each policy-level record sums its coverage across every classification, takes the largest classification minimum and adds MinPremium − CoveragePremium when the minimum is higher. Oklahoma's countrywide minimum rows for these keys are 0, so no top-up arises here.

8. Limits

  • Countrywide edition, one state. Six GL states rewrite SetPremium, all of them terrorism (AK, FL, NY, TX, VA, VT) — so a state run of this scenario may price terrorism differently. Nothing else in the scenario is state-rewritten.
  • Engine-rated, not ISO-confirmed at 2027-04-01. The figures are the engine's. The engine matches ISO on 50 of 50 standard risks and 184 of 184 varied risks, but all on pre-2027 editions; the 2027-04-01 numbers await a live call (docs/PRD-GL-RATING-ENGINE.md §8, C8).
  • Liquor dollars not shown. Oklahoma files no liquor rates in the countrywide tables used here; see LiquorLiability_RatingAlgorithms.md for a rated liquor risk.
  • Conditions flattened. Each rule's branches are summarised; a live rating takes one path.
