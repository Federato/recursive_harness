A worked scenario — how six coverages become one policy premium

The risk. One location, one classification (Mercantile), written on the Premises/Operations and Products/Completed Operations subline, with:

  • Premises/Operations coverage
  • Products/Completed Operations coverage
  • Liquor Liability
  • Medical Payments at $10,000
  • Terrorism (TRIA) accepted
  • Minimum premiums in play

Source: GL CW 20270401 V01. Companions: GL_Premium_Map.md (the whole line), GL_PremOps_Prods_Premium_Trace.md (the two core coverages in detail), GL_Premium_Tree.html (navigable).

Written: 2026-09-17.

1. First correction: that is five premium-bearing things, not six

Medical Payments does not produce a premium. There is a GeneralLiabilityMedPayCoverage record, and it does sit in the roll-up — but its rule file contains only InitializeRuleSet, CalculateTotalPremium and ErcProcess. No SetPremium, no rating logic of any kind. The one thing that ever writes its Premium is a line in ErcProcess that forces it to 0.0 when the coverage is off.

Medical Payments is priced as a factor inside the Premises/Operations rate:

if MedPayCoverage/Limit = "5,000":            MedicalPaymentsFactor = 1.0     (the included limit)
otherwise:                                     MedicalPaymentsFactor = LookupIncreasedMedPayLimitFactor(
                                                    State|CW, ClassificationType, MedPayLimit )

and then, in SetFinalILF on the Prem/Ops coverage:

FinalILF = round( (CSLILF + MedicalPaymentsFactor − 1) − FinalDeductibleFactor )

At $10,000, Mercantile, the countrywide factor is 1.007 — so Med Pay adds 0.007 to the ILF, roughly a 0.7% uplift on the Premises/Operations rate and nothing else. Contracting or Servicing pays 1.011 for the same limit.

Increased Med Pay limit Most classification types Contracting or Servicing
10,000 1.007 1.011
15,000 1.011 1.017
20,000 1.014 —

Consequences worth knowing. Med Pay never appears as a line item. It touches Premises/Operations only — not Products, not Liquor. And because it lands inside FinalILF, it is inside the terrorism base too (§4).

2. The five premiums, and where each is computed

# Coverage DataDef group Depth Computed by
1 Premises/Operations GeneralLiabilityClassificationPremOpsCoverage 3 SetPremium
2 Products/Completed Ops GeneralLiabilityClassificationProdsCompldOpsCoverage 3 SetPremium
3 Liquor Liability GeneralLiabilityClassificationLiquorCoverage 3 SetPremium
4 Terrorism — Prem/Ops GeneralLiabilityTerrorismPremOpsCoverage 4 SetPremium
5 Terrorism — Prods/CompldOps GeneralLiabilityTerrorismProdsCompldOpsCoverage 4 SetPremium
(5b) Terrorism — all other sublines GeneralLiabilityTerrorismAllOtherSublineCoverage 4 SetPremium — this is where Liquor's terrorism charge lands

Coverages 1–3 are siblings under the classification. The terrorism coverages sit one level deeper, under a GeneralLiabilityTerrorism record that is itself a child of the classification.

1–3 share one formula

BaseRate  = LossCost × LCM
FinalRate = BaseRate × FinalILF × SizeOfRisk × PackageMod
                     × ExperienceMod × ExpenseMod × ModToUse
Premium   = round( FinalRate × Exposure / 1000 , 0 )

Only the field names differ — PremOpsCovExposure, ProdsCompldOpsCovExposure, LiquorExposure. Liquor has no size-of-risk term.

The $1 floor. If the arithmetic gives 0 while exposure is above 0, Premium is forced to 1.0. Zero is reserved for "not covered".

3. Terrorism is a percentage of the other premiums

This is the part that makes the scenario order-dependent.

SetClassCoveragePremium on the terrorism coverage sums the premiums of the underlying coverages for that subline — Prem/Ops, Loss of Electronic Data, Cyber Incident Liability and the rest — then:

Premium = ClassCoveragePremium × TerrorismILF × exposure-class factors
                                × NuclearBiologicalChemicalRadiological factor

So terrorism cannot be computed until coverages 1–3 are final. It is a charge on the premium, not a parallel one. Three consequences:

  1. The Med Pay uplift is inside the terrorism base. A higher Med Pay limit raises Prem/Ops, which raises ClassCoveragePremium, which raises the terrorism charge. The $10,000 election costs slightly more than the 0.7% it appears to.
  2. Liquor's terrorism goes to a different record — …TerrorismAllOtherSublineCoverage, not the Prem/Ops one.
  3. TerrorismCoverage must be accepted at policy level — SetPremium reads it seven levels up. Declined, the charge is zero and the record still exists.

4. Minimum premium — a slot ISO leaves for you to fill

Each of the three coverages owns a minimum-premium lookup — LookupPremOpsMinPremium, LookupProdsCompldOpsMinPremium, LookupLiquorMinPremium, plus Prem/Ops's LookupSpecialCombinedMinPremium. And there are three dedicated records at policy level:

  • GeneralLiabilityPremOpsPremiumToReachMinCoverage
  • GeneralLiabilityProdsCompldOpsPremiumToReachMinCoverage
  • GeneralLiabilityLiquorPremiumToReachMinCoverage

Each is a leaf at depth 1 that is summed into the account total.

But nothing computes what goes in them. Their rule files hold only InitializeRuleSet, CalculateTotalPremium, SetCoverageOnPolicyIndicator and ErcProcess — no SetPremium. The only write to Premium forces it to 0.0 when the coverage is off. The related datadefs PremiumToReachMin and TotPremiumPriorToMin are, corpus-wide, written 0 times and read 0 times.

So ISO gives you the minimum premium, a place to put the shortfall, and a roll-up that will add it — but not the comparison in between. Deciding that the premium fell short, computing the top-up and supplying it is the caller's job. A submission that ignores these records produces a total with no minimum applied, and nothing flags it.

   depth 3         depth 3           depth 3                      ▲  depth 3
   ▲                                                   ┌──────────┼──────────┐
   │                                              Terr PremOps  Terr Prods  Terr AllOther
   └── Med Pay $10,000 enters here,               depth 4       depth 4     depth 4
       as +0.007 on FinalILF                           (each = ClassCoveragePremium × factors)

Order of computation:

  1. Rate and price Prem/Ops, Products and Liquor — Med Pay's 1.007 is inside Prem/Ops's ILF.
  2. Sum those into ClassCoveragePremium per subline; compute the three terrorism charges.
  3. Roll the terrorism record up into the classification alongside 1–3.
  4. Classification → location → policy.
  5. Separately, the caller compares each subline's premium against its minimum and puts any shortfall into the matching …PremiumToReachMinCoverage record at policy level, which the policy total adds directly.

Every contribution passes the same gate: a coverage's premium counts only when PremiumIndicator = 1 and Premium ≠ 0. A coverage priced at zero and a coverage never bought are indistinguishable in the total.

6. What is computed, and what you must supply

Computed by ISO's rules Supplied by the caller
Prem/Ops, Products, Liquor premium ✔ exposures, class code, limits, deductible
Med Pay charge ✔ (as an ILF factor) the Med Pay limit
Terrorism premium ✔ TerrorismCoverage acceptance
Package / experience / expense mods — all four, at policy level
Loss cost multiplier — the carrier's LCM
Minimum premium top-up the minimum only the comparison and the top-up amount

7. Limits

  • Countrywide. Six GL states rewrite SetPremium, all of them terrorism — so a state run of this scenario may price terrorism differently. Nothing else in the scenario is state-rewritten.
  • Structural. Read from rule XML; nothing was rated. No dollar figures are asserted beyond the filed Med Pay factors.
  • Conditions flattened. Each rule's branches are summarised; a live rating takes one path.
