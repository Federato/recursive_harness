A per-drone premium built from a weight band, three modifiers and Premises tables

Each designated drone is rated twice, once for Coverage A and once for Coverage B, per aircraft, with no exposure divisor. The loss cost comes from a countrywide weight-band table. Three modifiers then apply: ownership and operation, primary place of operation, and usage. LCM, claims-made, ILF and deductible factors are borrowed from the Premises/Operations tables, not from Premises' computed values.

Rule 37.C.2 says every loss cost and modifier "must be referred to company before using". The ERC numbers are reference values. All 51 jurisdictions are on pre-2027 today, and 46 move on 2027-04-01. A standard drone prices the same in both editions.

Coverage A · BI/PD
5 bands

Loss cost by maximum takeoff weight: up to 1, 5, 15 and 55 lb, and over 55 lb, which is 0 (refer). A deductible factor comes off the ILF.

Coverage B · PAI
2 bands

One loss cost up to 55 lb, and 0 above it. No deductible term. The same usage can price differently from Coverage A: firefighting is a referral for A but 0.90 for B.

Zero modifiers
6 ways

Unknown, Not Applicable, a referral usage, a blank usage, a weight over 55 lb, or a wrong-case 2027 key. Each prices the drone at $0, and ERC itself tests none of them.

What to notice

  • The weight ceiling rule (SetmaximumTakeoffWeightCeiling) is empty. Its logic moved into a helper, round(weight + 0.499, 0), called from the loss-cost rule.
  • The ILF is the Premises table with the table assignment fixed at 3. The limits are chosen from the drone's aggregate, the policy occurrence limit and the policy general aggregate.
  • The manual wants the highest modifier when more than one category applies on an axis. ERC takes one submitted value per axis, so that is a submission requirement (OI-48).
Referrals. ERC multiplies a 0 modifier straight into the rate. The engine's R19 detector, in underwriting mode only, refers when a drone lookup returns 0. It also fires on every drone, because the filed minimum premium is a 0 lookup (a false positive). It misses the blank-usage and wrong-case paths, which write a constant or miss the lookup.

What to notice

  • Ownership and place each have 9 rows, with 0 on "Unknown", "Not Applicable" and, for ownership, non-owned aircraft operated by others. Usage has 12 rows and no Unknown row. Five usages are referrals for Coverage A, and three for Coverage B.
  • The premium is one product rounded once to whole dollars. Nothing is rounded between the ILF and the final premium.
  • LCM, claims-made, ILF and deductible are each looked up separately by A and B. They share tables, not computed values.
Silent $0 drones. Over 55 lb, any 0 modifier, or a blank usage all price the drone at $0 with no ISO message. Underwriting mode refers through R19, except on blank usage. A weight of 0 also prices $0, but ISO messages it.

What to notice

  • The floor tests AdjustedRate > 0, not exposure. A drone the modifiers zero out still prices $0, but one whose ILF fails prices $1.
  • "Automatic Coverage" is a new option that switches these designated-drone coverages off. These two rule files do not price it.
  • The modifier tables re-case several labels. Case-sensitive keys mean a pre-2027 spelling silently misses.
New finding, 2026-10-05. A drone aggregate still carrying " CSL" misses every 2027 ILF row. The engine reads the null as 0 and the $1 floor applies, so Coverage A and B each price at $1 instead of 316 and 216, with no message. A pre-2027 usage spelling prices the drone at $0, also silently.

Eight differences across four rule files

Coverage A and B each go from 33 and 29 rules to 35 and 31 (one helper removed, three converters added). The policy file goes from 47 rules to 41. A standard drone prices the same in both editions.
