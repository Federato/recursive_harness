Per-coverage totals — where they exist

The bracket above aggregates by record hierarchy: classification, then location, then policy. It never aggregates by coverage. With three classifications across two locations there are three Prem/Ops Premium values and, on an ordinary policy, no field anywhere holding their sum.

ISO does compute per-coverage account totals — in two places, both conditional, and neither of them on the path the bracket draws.

Read that carefully before concluding ISO does not total by coverage. A search over datadef field names says it does not. That is wrong — the totals are rules that return values without ever writing a field, so a field-name search cannot see them. What is true is narrower: per-coverage totals are computed only under composite rating, or where a coverage needs one to test a minimum premium. On the ordinary policy the bracket draws, no per-coverage total is produced or stored.

Two related figures do sit on GeneralLiability and are easy to mistake for the answer: AnnualBasicLimitsCoPremiumPremOps and AnnualBasicLimitsCoPremiumProdCompldOps. They are built from BasicLimitPremium, not Premium — basic-limits sums for statistical reporting, a different quantity from what the coverage cost.

Three things the bracket shows

1 · One subline per risk. Every coverage on a classification is switched on or off by the policy’s Subline. Prem/Ops and Products share one risk; Liquor, OCP, Railroad and the rest each need their own GeneralLiability risk, and the account premium is the sum of the risks’ totals (engine: two copies of 1A’s risk rate to exactly 2 × 852 = 1,704).

2 · The policy-level leaves bypass the location chain — and in this edition they are all zero. Med Pay and the three PremiumToReachMin records attach directly to GeneralLiability, so they are added after every location has been totalled. In the 2027-04-01 edition the three minimum-premium records’ SetCoverageOnPolicyIndicator writes 0 unconditionally, so ErcProcess forces their Premium to 0: a caller cannot put a top-up there (engine: a supplied Premium = 100 is wiped and the total stays 852). A minimum premium on this edition has to be applied outside ISO’s roll-up. In the editions in force today (before 2027-04-01) these records do compute the top-up themselves — MinPremium − CoveragePremium when the minimum is higher, under the rule’s own conditions.

3 · Every merge applies the same gate. A leaf contributes its Premium only when PremiumIndicator = 1 and Premium ≠ 0. MedPayCoverage.Premium sits at 0 because nothing ever computes it — in this edition Med Pay is priced as a factor inside the Prem/Ops FinalILF (1A: 2.01 + 1.007 − 1 = 2.017). Before 2027-04-01 it is a separate MedicalPaymentsCharge added into the Prem/Ops premium.
