Premises/Operations and Products/Completed Operations — full premium trace

What this traces: for GL's two core coverages, every premium value computed along the way, and which of them survive into the account total.

The headline: each coverage computes two premium figures, and only one of them is money. The other travels all the way to policy level and is never added to anything.

Source: GL CW 20270401 V01 — GeneralLiabilityClassificationPremOpsCoverageRules.Rule.xml and GeneralLiabilityClassificationProdsCompldOpsCoverageRules.Rule.xml. Companion: GL_Premium_Map.md (the whole line), GL_Premium_Tree.html (navigable).

Written: 2026-09-17.

Reconciled against the built engine: 2026-10-02 (commit 630ba2f) — every step below was re-read in GL CW 20270401 V02 and watched executing in the engine's trace (offline, strict-erc): Oklahoma at 2027-04-01 (GL_OK_20270401_V04 over GL_CW_20270401_V02) and the Oklahoma ISO golden case at 2026-08-01 (GL_OK_20260801_V01 over GL_CW_20260101_V01). The worked figures in §2a are the engine's. All 16 tests/verify_* suites pass.

Edition scope. This traces the 2027-04-01 countrywide edition (V01; V02 is identical for these two coverages). The editions in force for policies effective before 2027-04-01 (CW 20231201 V02/V03, 20260101 V01, 20261001 V01) run a longer chain: Prem/Ops 99 rules against 86, Products 89 against 81. The differences that move money: Med Pay is a separate per-class MedicalPaymentsCharge added into the Prem/Ops premium (LookupMedPayFactor) rather than a term inside FinalILF (LookupIncreasedMedPayLimitFactor); each coverage computes its own MinPremium and the policy-level …PremiumToReachMinCoverage records add a top-up (§4); BI/PD split-limit weights and SetBIILF/SetPDILF feed the ILF; and there is no $1 floor. Section-by-section notes below flag where this matters.

1. The two coverages are structurally identical

Both sit at depth 3, both are leaves, and both carry the same seven rules. Every difference between them is a substituted field name:

Premises/Operations Products/Completed Operations
Loss cost PremOpsLossCost ProdsCompldOpsLossCost
Company ELP PremOpsELP ProdsCompldOpsELP
Exposure ../PremOpsCovExposure ../ProdsCompldOpsCovExposure
Size-of-risk PremOpsSizeOfRiskFinalRelativity ProdsCompldOpsSizeOfRiskFinalRelativity
Minimum premium lookup LookupPremOpsMinPremium, LookupSpecialCombinedMinPremium LookupProdsCompldOpsMinPremium

Prem/Ops carries one extra rule — LookupSpecialCombinedMinPremium — and one extra base-rate branch for class codes 16905 / 16906, which apply a BringYourOwnAlcoholExclusionFactor. Otherwise the two files are the same program.

2. The chain, in order

Step 1 — SetBaseRate

BaseRate = round( LossCost × LCM , 3 )                 loss cost filed (≠ 0)
BaseRate = round( ELP × LCM , 3 )                      loss cost = 0  → the ELP path
         × ClaimsMadeMultiplier                        when the coverage form is Claims Made

The ELP path is selected by the loss cost being 0, not by a company choosing to file one: SetBaseRate tests LossCost = 0.0 and, if so, uses the ELP instead (the ELP rule itself returns 0 when the class's ELPText says "Rate/Loss Cost Applies"). Prem/Ops has the third branch for classes 16905/16906 when the Amendment of Liquor Liability Exclusion endorsement is attached (LossCost × BringYourOwnAlcoholExclusionFactor × LCM). Assigned only when currently null, so a supplied BaseRate survives. Engine: OK golden, class 50017 — Prem/Ops loss cost 0.093 → BaseRate 0.093; Products loss cost 0, so ELP 0.82 → BaseRate 0.820. A loss cost that resolves to null (no row in the state or CW table) is not a zero — the kernel refuses the rating (OI-94, Kernel._refuse_null_loss_cost).

Step 2 — SetFinalRate

FinalRate = round( BaseRate
          × FinalILF                              increased limits (the deductible credit is folded in here)
          × SizeOfRiskFinalRelativity             only when SizeOfRiskRatingApplies = Yes
          × PackageModFactor                      ┐
          × ExperienceRatingModificationFactor    │ all four read from policy level,
          × ExpenseModification                   │ five levels up
          × ModToUse                              ┘
          , 3 )

FinalILF is itself rounded to 3 places: CSLILF − FinalDeductibleFactor for Products; for Prem/Ops (CSLILF + MedicalPaymentsFactor − 1) − FinalDeductibleFactor when the Med Pay limit is anything but the included "5,000" and no Coverage C exclusion is attached, else CSLILF − FinalDeductibleFactor; a result ≤ 0 is written as 0. When size-of-risk does not apply the relativity fields are written 0.0 and simply left out of the product — they are not a multiplier of zero.

Step 3 — SetBasicLimitPremium ← the sub-premium

BasicLimitPremium = BaseRate
                  × (1 − FinalDeductibleFactor)
                  × SizeOfRiskFinalRelativity      (only when SizeOfRiskRatingApplies = Yes)
                  × PackageModFactor
                  × (Exposure / 1000)

Rounded to whole dollars, written only when the policy Subline is Premises/Operations and Products/Completed Operations (otherwise 0), and undivided by 1,000 for the bases that are not per-thousand — the same split SetPremium makes.

Note what is absent: FinalILF, the experience mod, the expense mod and ModToUse. This is deliberate — it is the basic limits premium, the figure before increased limits and before the account's own modifications. It is a different quantity from the premium, not an intermediate step toward it.

Step 4 — SetPremium ← the money

Premium = round( FinalRate × Exposure / 1000 , 0 )

for the nine per-thousand bases (Admissions, Area, Gallons, Gross Sales, Kilowatt-hours, Payroll, Total Cost, Total Operating Expenses, Vehicles); otherwise FinalRate × Exposure undivided. Rounded to whole dollars at the coverage, not at the total.

A floor almost nobody knows about. If the calculation produces 0 while exposure is greater than 0, the rule writes Premium = 1.0. A real exposure can never rate to nothing — it rates to one dollar. This matters because the roll-up treats 0 as "no coverage" (§3), so the floor is what keeps a real-but-tiny risk visible. The floor is new in the 2027-04-01 edition — the CW 20231201–20261001 SetPremium rules have no such branch, so before that date a tiny exposure does round to 0 (engine: a $21 Products premium's terrorism charge, 21 × 0.004 = 0.084, is 1 at 2027-04-01 and 0 at 2026-10-02).

Before 2027-04-01 SetPremium also adds the Prem/Ops coverage's MedicalPaymentsCharge (see Edition scope).

Step 5 — SetPremiumIndicator

PremiumIndicator = 1 if Premium ≠ 0, else 0

Gate — SetCoverageOnPolicyIndicator

Prem/Ops: 1 only when the policy's Subline is Premises/Operations and Products/Completed Operations. Products: 1 for that subline unless ProdsCompldOpsExclusion = Yes, and also for the Products/Completed Operations subline on its own. If the gate is 0, ErcProcess writes Premium = 0 and none of the steps above runs.

3. What survives, and what does not

Premium survives. BasicLimitPremium does not — but it still travels.

The money track

Premium  (coverage, depth 3)
   │   CalculateTotalPremium: contribute Premium only if
   │   PremiumIndicator = 1 AND Premium ≠ 0, else contribute 0
   ▼
GeneralLiabilityClassification.ErcCalculatedTotalPremium      depth 2
   │   summed with its 18 sibling children — Terrorism, Liquor, Pollution,
   │   Cyber Prem/Ops and Prods, Loss of Electronic Data, Railroad,
   │   Owners & Contractors, Underground Storage Tank, Snowplow,
   │   Unmanned Aircraft, Med Pay Exclusion C, and the classification's own Premium
   ▼
GeneralLiabilityLocation.ErcCalculatedTotalPremium            depth 1
   ▼
GeneralLiability.ErcCalculatedTotalPremium                    depth 0  ← THE ACCOUNT TOTAL

Three summations from coverage to account. Nothing re-reads a stored subtotal — each level passes its value up as a RunRule return, so the stored ErcCalculatedTotalPremium at each level is an output for the caller, not an input to the level above.

The statistical track

BasicLimitPremium is read nine times — five times inside Prem/Ops, four inside Prods/Completed Ops, and four times at policy level, by exactly two rules:

Rule (on GeneralLiability) Writes
SetAnnualBasicLimitsCoPremiumPremOps AnnualBasicLimitsCoPremiumPremOps
SetAnnualBasicLimitsCoPremiumProdCompldOps AnnualBasicLimitsCoPremiumProdCompldOps

Both read BasicLimitPremium and the Subline, and write a policy-level annual basic-limits figure. Neither feeds CalculateTotalPremium. The basic-limits premium reaches the top of the policy and lands in its own field, for statistical and company reporting — it is never added to what the insured pays.

So the answer to "which sub-premiums persist": one does, and it persists into a parallel figure rather than into the total.

4. Minimum premium — looked up, and nothing more

Both coverages own minimum-premium lookups: LookupPremOpsMinPremium, LookupProdsCompldOpsMinPremium, and Prem/Ops's LookupSpecialCombinedMinPremium. Two related datadefs exist on every record — PremiumToReachMin and TotPremiumPriorToMin.

Measured across the corpus: both are written 0 times and read 0 times. MinPremium itself is written 4 times and read 10, but every reader is a different coverage — …CyberIncidentLiabilityPremiumToReachMinCoverage, …LossOfElectronicDataPremiumToReachMinCoverage, …LimitedProductWithdrawalExpenseEndtPremiumToReachMinCoveragePolLvl.

Neither Prem/Ops nor Prods/Completed Ops applies a minimum to its own premium. The lookups resolve a value that those two coverages never consume — and in the engine's 2027-04-01 runs they are not even invoked: ErcRate no longer calls them. The policy-level GeneralLiabilityPremOpsPremiumToReachMinCoverage and …ProdsCompldOps… records are switched off in this edition (SetCoverageOnPolicyIndicator writes 0 unconditionally, so ErcProcess forces their Premium to 0 — a caller-supplied top-up is wiped; engine-tested). Minimum-premium enforcement for this pair, on this edition, is not in the filed content and cannot be routed through ISO's roll-up.

Before 2027-04-01 it is. In CW 20231201 V02/V03, 20260101 V01 and 20261001 V01 each coverage runs SetMinimumPremium → SetMinPremium (Products: round(MinimumPremium × FinalILF × AdditionalInterestFactor, 0)), and the policy-level records compute CoveragePremium = Σ of that coverage's classification premiums, MinPremium = the largest classification minimum, and Premium = MinPremium − CoveragePremium when the minimum is higher (subject to the rule's own package/multistate/exposure conditions), else 0 — a real, ISO-computed top-up. Engine: OK @ 2026-08-01, CoveragePremium 996 / 7,215, MinPremium 0 (the CW minimum tables hold 0 for these keys), top-up 0.

5. Summary

Value Where computed Survives to the account total?
BaseRate coverage no — a rate, consumed by FinalRate and BasicLimitPremium
FinalRate coverage no — a rate, consumed by Premium
BasicLimitPremium coverage no — reaches policy level as AnnualBasicLimitsCoPremium…, a reported figure
Premium coverage yes — the only value that becomes money, via three summations
PremiumIndicator coverage not a premium — the gate that decides whether Premium counts
MinPremium coverage 2027 edition: no — never applied. Earlier editions: compared at policy level, the shortfall added as a top-up
PremiumToReachMin, TotPremiumPriorToMin — never written, never read

6. Limits

  • Countrywide. Six states rewrite SetPremium on GL, all of them Terrorism, so neither coverage traced here is affected. State packages were not re-traced rule by rule; the engine applies whatever a state overrides automatically.
  • Evaluated, not just structural (since 2026-10-02). §2a's figures are engine output. The 2026-08-01 column equals ISO's own priced output; the 2027-04-01 figures are the engine's and have not yet been confirmed against ISO's live service (docs/PRD-GL-RATING-ENGINE.md §8, C8). The engine's wider ISO agreement — 50 of 50 standard risks (scripts/erc/out/phase2.csv) and 184 of 184 varied risks in 11 jurisdictions (BUILD-LOG Entry 24) — is on pre-2027 editions.
  • Conditions flattened. Each rule's branches are summarised; a live rating takes one path through them, not all.
