GL premium map — every premium field, what feeds it, where it ends up

The question this answers: for General Liability, where does every premium number come from, and how does it reach the one figure that is the account's premium?

The answer in one line: GeneralLiability.ErcCalculatedTotalPremium is the account total, and every one of the 571 groups that carry a premium reaches it — through at most four levels of summation.

Source: GL CW 20270401 V01, countrywide, 572 rule files. Navigable version: GL_Algorithm/GL_Premium_Tree.html — the same tree, collapsible and searchable, with a jurisdiction selector that overlays what each of the 51 state packages adds or rewrites.

Companion data: GL_Algorithm/GL_Premium_Map.xlsx — four sheets: Summary, RollUpTree (571 rows, one per group), PremiumSources (322 rows, one per premium calculation), PremiumFields (163 rows).

Written: 2026-09-17.

1. Three mechanisms, and only three

The whole premium architecture is three rule names repeated across the corpus:

Rule Count What it does
SetPremium 322 turns a rate into dollars on one record
SetPremiumIndicator 322 says whether that record produced a premium at all
CalculateTotalPremium 571 collects premium upward, one level at a time

There is no fourth mechanism and no special case at the top. The account total is produced by the same rule name as every subtotal beneath it.

2. Rate and premium are separate phases

Not one of the 322 SetPremium rules performs a table lookup. Checked directly: zero MatrixDef references across all of them.

By the time SetPremium runs, the rating chain has already resolved the record's FinalRate and written it to the record. SetPremium only converts. The canonical case, GeneralLiabilityClassificationPremOpsCoverage, reads exactly four values:

FinalRate                  the rate, already computed
PremiumBasis               which divisor applies (Admissions, Area, Gross Sales, Payroll …)
../PremOpsCovExposure      the exposure, from the parent classification
../Subline                 which subline is in force

and uses two operators — Product and Divide. That is the entire premium calculation: rate × exposure ÷ basis divisor.

This split matters for anyone reading a wrong premium: a wrong number is either a wrong rate (the rating chain) or a wrong exposure/basis (the premium chain). The two are cleanly separable, and FinalRate is the seam.

3. How a premium climbs to the account total

At a leaf — a coverage record

CalculateTotalPremium on a leaf is a gate, not a sum:

if PremiumIndicator = 1 AND Premium <> 0:
        contribute Premium
else:
        contribute 0

Two conditions, not one. A record with a premium but an indicator of 0 contributes nothing, and so does a record whose premium is exactly zero. PremiumIndicator is what distinguishes "this coverage rated to zero" from "this coverage was not on the policy" — and both contribute the same 0, so the indicator is the only place that difference survives.

At an aggregator — a location, a classification, the policy

ErcCalculatedTotalPremium = Sum(
    for each child record:  that child's CalculateTotalPremium
)

Children are reached two different ways, and reading only one of them silently loses premium:

Child kind How it is summed Payload form
repeating <rul:ForEach AtDataDef="XTable/X"> JSON array
singleton <rul:Locate AtDataDef="X"> JSON object

This is the same array-vs-object distinction that governs the request format. At the root, 400 children are repeating and 19 are singletons — a reader that handled only ForEach would miss 19 whole endorsement families.

The spine

GeneralLiability                                   depth 0   419 children   ← ACCOUNT TOTAL
└── GeneralLiabilityLocation                       depth 1    18 children
    └── GeneralLiabilityClassification             depth 2    19 children
        ├── …PremOpsCoverage                       depth 3   leaf, SetPremium
        ├── …ProdsCompldOpsCoverage                depth 3   leaf, SetPremium
        ├── GeneralLiabilityTerrorism              depth 3    3 children
        │   └── …TerrorismPremOpsCoverage          depth 4   leaf, SetPremium
        └── GeneralLiabilityUnmannedAircraft       depth 3    3 children
            └── …UnmannedAircraftCovABIPDCoverage  depth 4   leaf, SetPremium

Location and Classification contribute their own Premium as well as their children's — they are not pure containers. A premium can be written at any level.

Depth distribution

Depth below the account total Groups
0 — the account itself 1
1 419
2 110
3 34
4 — deepest 7

The tree is wide, not deep. 419 of 571 groups hang directly off the policy — almost all of them endorsements that price at policy level rather than against a classification. Only 7 groups sit four levels down: the three Terrorism coverages, the three Unmanned Aircraft coverages, and Medical Payments Exclusion Coverage C.

Every group reaches the root. Zero orphans — no group computes a premium that the account total fails to collect.

4. The premium fields themselves

163 distinct premium-named fields exist in the DataDef. Only 22 are ever written by a rule. The other 141 are carried, not computed.

Twelve of them are declared on all 1,025 datadef groups — the standard premium block every record carries whether or not it ever holds a premium:

Premium · PremiumIndicator · AnnualPremium · PolicyTermPremium · ProRatedPremium · PriorPremium · ProRatedPriorPremium · ChangePremium · ProRatedChangePremium · TotalProRatedChangePremium · EvaluatedPremium · OverridePremium

Of those twelve, the rules write exactly two — Premium and PremiumIndicator. The other ten are policy-administration fields, and §5 measures just how inert they are: zero writes and zero reads across all 52 jurisdictions. A reader who assumes AnnualPremium is computed will find nothing computing it, in any state.

What the rules actually write

Field Writes Role
PremiumIndicator 644 did this record produce a premium
Premium 393 the record's own premium
ErcCalculatedTotalPremium 112 a subtotal, at each aggregating level
BasicLimitPremium 9 pre-ILF premium
PremiumDiscountCharge 6
PremiumBasis 6
MinPremium 4
the remaining 15 1–3 each coverage-specific totals

ErcCalculatedTotalPremium being written 112 times against 571 participating groups is the aggregator count: 112 groups hold a subtotal, 459 are leaves that hand their Premium upward without storing one.

5. Interim premium — written, pro-rata, endorsement: not in the ruleset

A fair question about any premium map is whether it covers the transaction side: written premium, pro-rata, earned, endorsement change. It does not, because ISO's ruleset does not compute any of it. That is a finding, not an omission in the map — and it was checked rather than assumed.

Every record in the corpus carries a fourteen-field policy-administration block, declared on all 1,025 datadef groups. Measured across all 52 jurisdictions, every one of the fourteen is written zero times and read zero times by any rule:

Field Writes Reads
AnnualPremium · PolicyTermPremium · PriorPremium 0 0
ProRatedPremium · ProRatedPriorPremium · ProRatedChangePremium · TotalProRatedChangePremium 0 0
ChangePremium · EvaluatedPremium · OverridePremium 0 0
ProRateFactor · ProRateIndicator · TermFactorIndicator · EarnedInFullIndicator 0 0

Nothing computes them; nothing consumes them. They are inert carriers — a shape a policy administration system is expected to fill, shipped inside ISO's schema so a round-tripped record has somewhere to put them.

What this means in practice. The engine produces one number: the full-term premium at the effective date supplied in SchemeKeys. Proration, mid-term endorsement pricing, written-vs-earned splits and cancellation return arithmetic are entirely the calling system's job. Re-rating at a new effective date and differencing the results is the only transaction mechanism ISO's content offers, and even that is the caller's construction.

One name that looks like an exception and is not. EndorsementPremium is live — one write, two reads. But it sits on GeneralLiabilityTerrorismEndorsementCoverage, and SetEndorsementPremium sums the terrorism contributions of ~40 endorsement coverages. It means "premium for the terrorism endorsement", not "premium change from an endorsement transaction". Same word, unrelated concept — the same shared-name trap this project keeps hitting.

Also worth knowing: ErcCalculatedTotalPremium is written 200 times across the corpus and read zero times. The roll-up passes values through RunRule return values, never by reading the stored subtotal. Each level's stored total is an output for the caller, not an input to the level above — so a caller may treat every subtotal as reportable without worrying that something downstream depends on it.

6. Reading this for a specific premium

To trace any number on a GL result:

  1. Find the group in RollUpTree. Its Path to account total column is the full chain of summations between that record and the account figure.
  2. If it is a leaf, its Premium came from SetPremium — look it up in PremiumSources for the exact fields that rule reads.
  3. If it is an aggregator, its ErcCalculatedTotalPremium is the sum of its children plus, where marked, its own Premium.
  4. If a premium is missing rather than wrong, check PremiumIndicator before checking the arithmetic. A zero contribution and an absent coverage are indistinguishable in the total; the indicator is the only discriminator.

7. What the state packages do to this

The countrywide tree is the shape; the 51 state packages change it in two ways and, notably, never in a third.

They add. 457 distinct premium-bearing groups exist only in state packages — state endorsements, each arriving with its own CalculateTotalPremium so it rolls up like any other child. The heaviest: WA 33, IL 27, MO 26, NY 25, NC 24, NH 23; the lightest, WV with 4. Every one of the 780 state CalculateTotalPremium rules is scoped RuleTypeSystem — machinery that comes with the group, not a rewrite of countrywide's.

They rewrite, but barely, and only in one place. Exactly 12 SetPremium overrides exist across all 51 states, and all 12 are Terrorism:

Group States CW size State size
…TerrorismAllOtherSublineCoverage AK, FL, NY, TX, VA, VT 40,846 17,214 – 42,613
…TerrorismPremOpsCoverage FL, NY 10,549 2,725 – 3,339
…TerrorismProdsCompldOpsCoverage FL, NY 11,144 2,742 – 3,635
…UnmannedAircraftTerrorismCoverage FL, NY 12,667 2,861 – 3,470

FL and NY replace the terrorism premium calculation with rules a quarter to a fifth the size — substantially different logic, not a tweak. AK's is slightly larger than countrywide's. No other countrywide premium formula is rewritten by any state.

They never delete. Zero empty-body premium overrides anywhere in the corpus. This is worth stating because it is the opposite of CF's validation messages, where 316 countrywide rules are switched off by a state override with an empty body. Premium rules are only ever added to or replaced — never silently removed.

8. Limits of this map

  • Countrywide only. State packages override rules, and a state may override SetPremium or CalculateTotalPremium — none of that is reflected here. The roll-up shape is unlikely to differ; the premium formulas may.
  • Structural, not evaluated. The tree is read from the rule XML. No premium was computed, and no path was exercised against a live rating.
  • SetPremium inputs are the fields the rule reads, flattened from nested conditions. A rule reading eight fields may only need two in any given branch.
  • FinalRate is where this map stops. How a rate becomes FinalRate is the rating chain, which is a separate body of work — this map covers the rate-to-dollars half and the aggregation above it.
